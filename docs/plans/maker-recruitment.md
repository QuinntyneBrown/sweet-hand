# Plan: find 100 makers by November 6

**Status:** Founder's plan, 12 October 2026. Not a specification. This is a
field plan for the first number in the [business plan](business-plan.md): 100
home cooks and small-batch makers on the directory before launch. Nothing here
changes the product; the sign-up path is the launch list that already exists at
`https://sweethand.ca` (L2-092 to L2-107).

## The short answer

**Do not knock on house doors. Knock on shop doors, church doors and WhatsApp
doors.** A person selling oxtail out of their kitchen is invisible from the
street, and a stranger at the door asking "do you sell food from home?" sounds
like an inspector. Door-to-door at homes would cost you a whole Saturday for
one or two sellers, if that. You have 25 days and need four makers a day, so
every hour has to go where sellers already gather or already advertise:
Instagram, Facebook groups, WhatsApp, Caribbean grocers, roti shops, salons and
barbershops, churches, and the makers you already know, each of whom knows two
more.

The plan below gets to 100 by stacking five channels, with referrals carrying
the back half. It assumes you work it most days, in person on the weekends.

## What "found" means

Count a maker only when all five are true, so the number is real:

1. A name and a way to reach them (WhatsApp number or Instagram handle).
2. What they cook and which tradition (Jamaican, Trini, Guyanese, Bajan,
   Haitian, Grenadian, Vincentian, Lucian, and so on; their words, not yours).
3. The area they sell from (neighbourhood or municipality, never the address).
4. A **yes** to being listed free when Sweet Hand opens.
5. They are on the launch list as a caterer at `sweethand.ca`, or you entered
   their yes in the tracker yourself with the date.

Three bars, in order of strength:

| Bar | Target by Nov 6 | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Found and said yes (the five points above) | **100** | The number this plan is for |
| Studio shoot booked (the free 10-dish shoot from the business plan) | 50 | Proof the yes is real; starts filling the photo library |
| Profile complete and photographed | 25 | Launch day on Nov 26 needs these live first |

Plan for 130 to 150 yeses. Some will go quiet; a directory that shows "100"
on launch day needs slack.

## Dates

25 days from Thanksgiving Monday (Oct 12) to Friday Nov 6. Two seasonal hooks
fall inside the window and should be in every pitch:

- **Christmas starts now.** Black cake fruit is soaking, sorrel and ginger beer
  orders open in November, and every seller is looking for customers for
  December. "Be listed before the Christmas rush" is the line.
- **Diwali is November 8.** Indo-Caribbean makers (Trini and Guyanese kurma,
  barfi, parsad, sweet rice, mithai boxes) take orders in the last week of
  October and the first week of November. Reach them the week of Oct 26.

| Week | Dates | Cumulative yes | Focus |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Mon Oct 12 – Sun Oct 18 | 20 | Set up; own network; build the Instagram list; post in groups |
| 1 | Mon Oct 19 – Sun Oct 25 | 45 | DM volume; grocer and roti shop round; first church Sunday |
| 2 | Mon Oct 26 – Sun Nov 1 | 75 | Referrals compounding; Diwali makers; first studio shoots |

### 1. Your network and the referral chain (35)

The business plan's own estimate is that every Caribbean household knows one
or two sellers. That is the cheapest source you have, and it is the only one
that compounds.

- **Today, Thanksgiving Monday.** Ask every family member and friend at the
  table: "Who do you buy from? Who makes your black cake? Who did the food at
  the last christening?" Write every name down. Expect 10 to 20 names from the
  weekend alone.
- **Every yes is asked for two names.** The last question of every
  conversation, without exception: "Who else cooks that I should talk to?"
  Then "Can I say you sent me?" A warm intro converts at roughly half; a cold
  message at a seventh.
- **Referral incentive.** The business plan gives a referring maker a month of
  Maker Pro. Pro does not exist yet, so before launch the incentive is
  concrete: the referrer's dishes get shot first, and their listing goes live
  on day one.
- **Caterers you have photographed.** Anyone you have shot an event for, and
  every caterer at those events, gets a message this week.

### 2. Makers already on Instagram and TikTok (35)

This is where most GTA home sellers already advertise. They post a plate, say
"DM to order", and sell to whoever follows them. Your pitch is that Sweet Hand
is the search page they do not have.

**Build the list (week 0, 4 hours).** Open a spreadsheet and collect 250
accounts. Search hashtags and location tags, then follow the trail of who
each account follows and who comments on them:

- Dish tags: `#jerkchickentoronto`, `#rotitoronto`, `#doublestoronto`,
  `#oxtailtoronto`, `#blackcake`, `#sorrel`, `#pepperpot`, `#pelau`,
  `#curryduck`, `#saltfish`, `#kurma`, `#pholourie`, `#jamaicanpatties`
- Place tags: `#scarboroughfood`, `#bramptonfood`, `#ajaxfood`,
  `#pickeringfood`, `#mississaugafood`, `#oshawafood`, `#torontocaribbeanfood`
- Identity tags: `#caribbeancaterertoronto`, `#jamaicancanadian`,
  `#trinisintoronto`, `#guyaneseintoronto`, `#bajan`, `#haitiantoronto`
- Phrases in bios and captions: "DM to order", "pre-orders open", "taking
  orders", "home cooked", "catering available", "cash app / e-transfer"

Qualify each account in ten seconds: cooks from home or small scale, sells in
the GTA, posted in the last 60 days. Skip restaurants and franchises.

**Send 20 to 30 new DMs a day** from the Sweet Hand account (the Instagram kit
in `docs/marketing/instagram/` has the bio and first posts; the account must
look real before you DM from it, so post the three launch images first).
Instagram throttles accounts that send many cold messages, so stay under 30 a
day and follow and like a post before messaging. Expect a third to reply.

**Reply within the hour** when they write back. Move the conversation to
WhatsApp or a call; a yes almost never happens inside Instagram.

### 3. Facebook groups, Marketplace and WhatsApp groups (25)

Facebook is where the older sellers and the buyers are, and the groups already
do what Sweet Hand will do: "anyone know who sells good roti in Ajax?"

- **Groups.** Search Facebook for Jamaicans in Toronto, Trinis in Toronto,
  Trinbagonians in Canada, Guyanese in Toronto, Bajans in Toronto, Haitians in
  Toronto, Grenadians in Toronto, Vincentians in Toronto, St. Lucians in
  Toronto, Caribbean buy and sell, Scarborough and Brampton and Ajax community
  groups, and West Indian food groups. Join 15 to 20. Message each admin
  first and ask permission to post; a founder asking politely usually gets a
  yes and sometimes a pin.
- **Post, then mine the comments.** Post the maker image from
  `docs/marketing/facebook/post-2-makers.png` with the group-post copy below.
  The post itself brings a handful of makers; the comments bring more, because
  members tag the people they buy from. Every tag is a warm lead: message the
  tagged person and say who tagged them.
- **Search old threads.** Search each group for "who sells", "black cake",
  "roti", "catering" and read the answers. Every name in those threads has
  already been recommended by a customer.
- **Marketplace and Kijiji.** Search "jerk", "roti", "black cake", "catering",
  "Caribbean food" with the GTA as the location. Sellers listing there are
  already looking for strangers to buy from them.
- **WhatsApp groups.** Ask the people in channel 1 which groups they are in
  (church, family, island association, workplace) and to forward the maker
  Status image from `docs/marketing/whatsapp/status-2-makers.png` with two
  lines in their own words. A forward from a trusted member outperforms any
  post you make yourself.

### 4. In person: shop doors, not house doors (20)

These are the doors to knock on. Sellers shop at Caribbean grocers, buy
containers from the same suppliers, and the owners know who buys ten pounds of
oxtail every Friday.

Do two rounds, Saturday Oct 24 and Saturday Oct 31. Each round is a full day
on one or two corridors:

| Corridor | Why | Round |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Eglinton West, Marlee to Keele (Little Jamaica) | The oldest Jamaican strip in the city; grocers, patty shops, salons, record shops | Oct 24 |
| Scarborough: Markham Rd, Lawrence East, Malvern, Kennedy and Eglinton | Largest Caribbean population in Toronto; Trini, Guyanese and Jamaican grocers and roti shops | Oct 24 |
| Jane and Finch, Rexdale, Weston Rd | Jamaican and Vincentian; community centres and churches | Oct 31 |
| Brampton: Queen St E, Bramalea, Chinguacousy | Guyanese and Trini; Diwali sweet makers this week | Oct 31 |
| Ajax and Pickering: Harwood, Kingston Rd | Fast-growing Jamaican and Trini community; fewer competitors for attention | Oct 31 |
| Mississauga (Malton) and Oshawa | If the first rounds run short of leads | Nov 2–4 |

At each stop:

1. Introduce yourself to the owner or the person at the till with a business
   card (`docs/business-cards/quinntyne-brown/`). One sentence: "I'm building
   a free directory of Caribbean home cooks; do you know people who sell from
   home?" Owners know, and they usually like the idea because sellers are
   their best customers.
2. Ask to leave a stack of flyers at the till and put sticker 2
   (`docs/marketing/stickers/`) on the door or the bulletin board. Both files
   are print-ready; order 500 flyers and 100 stickers from an online printer
   on Oct 13 so they arrive by Oct 22.
3. Ask for the bulletin board. Many grocers have one with handwritten "black
   cake orders" and "catering" cards. Photograph the board. Every card is a
   lead with a phone number on it.
4. Hit the salons and barbershops on the same block. They are where food
   sellers get recommended, and owners often sell food themselves on the
   side.

Flyers left on a counter bring a trickle. The conversation with the owner
brings the names. Spend your time on the conversation.

### 5. Churches, community centres and events (15)

- **Churches.** Pick four congregations with large Caribbean memberships
  (Pentecostal, Seventh-day Adventist, Anglican and Catholic parishes in
  Scarborough, Jane and Finch, Brampton and Ajax). Call the church office in
  week 0, ask to speak to the pastor or the hospitality committee, and ask
  for two minutes at the announcements or a table after the service on Oct
  25 and Nov 1. The women who run the church kitchen know every seller in the
  congregation.
- **Fall harvest and bazaar season.** Late October and early November are
  harvest festival, bazaar and bake-sale season in exactly these churches.
  Every table at a bake sale is a maker.
- **Community centres and associations.** Island associations (Jamaican
  Canadian Association, the Trinidad and Tobago and Guyanese and Vincentian
  associations and the like), Caribbean seniors' groups and the community
  centres in Malvern, Jane and Finch and Rexdale. Ask each one to share the
  flyer and to name their caterers.
- **Diwali prep week (Oct 26 – Nov 6).** Ask Brampton and Scarborough grocers
  and mandirs who is making sweets this year. Pitch those makers for Diwali
  now and Christmas after.
- **Halloween weekend markets and pop-ups.** Any Caribbean vendor at a
  market that weekend is a maker.

## The pitch

Twenty seconds, in plain words, with no promise you cannot keep:

> I'm Quinntyne. I'm building Sweet Hand, a free site where people in Toronto
> find Caribbean home cooks by dish, island and neighbourhood. It opens the
> end of November, right before Christmas orders. It's free to list, you set
> your own menu, batches and days, and your home address never shows, only
> your area. The first hundred makers get a free photo shoot of their dishes
> in my studio downtown. Can I put you on the list?

Then the two questions that make the number: "Which dishes should I write
down?" and "Who else cooks that I should talk to?"

**What you offer, and only this:**

- Free listing, free forever, no commission on the directory.
- Approximate area only; the address is never shown.
- A free 10-dish studio shoot for the first 100 (about 90 minutes, they bring
  the food, they keep the photos for their own Instagram).
- Their listing live on launch day, before the Christmas rush.

**What you never say:** that Sweet Hand certifies, inspects or approves
anyone's kitchen; that a listing is a licence; or that orders are guaranteed.

> Looking for the people who cook. I'm building Sweet Hand, a free directory

> mouth. It opens end of November, in time for black cake and sorrel season.
> Free to list, your home address never shows, and the first 100 makers get a
> free photo shoot of their dishes. If you sell, or you know who does, comment
> or tag them. sweethand.ca

### Objections you will hear

| They say | You say |
| --- | --- |
| "I don't have a licence" | "The site never says anyone is certified. You write your own food-safety note, in your words, or nothing. Your call." Keep a link to the public health unit's home-food guidance and the food handler course ready; it is their decision, not your pitch. |
| "I don't want my address out there" | "It never shows. You pick a neighbourhood or an intersection and that's all anyone sees. Pickup details go only to the customer you choose." |
| "I only cook for people I know" | "That's fine. You set the batches and the days; a listing with 'by pre-order, Fridays only' is exactly the point." |
| "What does it cost?" | "Nothing. The directory stays free. Later there's an optional paid tier; nobody has to take it." |
| "Why you?" | Show the video (`docs/videos/05-cater-with-sweet-hand/`) and the preview on your phone. Say you are a photographer and an engineer and this is your own work. |
| "Send me something" | Send the WhatsApp Status image and the link. Then follow up in 48 hours; "send me something" is a maybe, not a yes. |

## What to collect from each maker

Only this, in one private spreadsheet. No home addresses, ever. Keep it to
yourself; it is personal data.

```
date, name, business name, phone or whatsapp, instagram, tradition, dishes,
area, how they sell now, food handler note (their words, optional),
status (lead / contacted / replied / yes / shoot booked / on launch list),
referred by, referred (names they gave), next step, next step date
```

The `referred` column is the engine. Review it every evening and message
every new name the next morning.

## If it is going wrong

Check on Sunday Oct 25 and Sunday Nov 1.

| Signal | Do |
| --- | --- |
| Under 35 on Oct 25 | Drop the two weakest channels and spend the hours on referrals and Instagram. Raise the DM count to 30. Ask for three names instead of two. |
| Instagram replies under 20% | Fix the account first: more posts, a maker already shot in the studio pinned at the top, the video in the bio link. Then shorten the DM to three lines. |
| Many maybes, few yeses | The ask is too big. Stop pitching the profile; ask only for a yes to be told when it opens and a yes to the free shoot. Everything else can wait. |
| Group admins say no | Comment instead of post. Answer "who sells roti in Ajax?" threads with a real recommendation from your list, and let the makers see Sweet Hand there. |
| Flyers bring nothing | Expected. Flyers are for the owner conversation. Do not reprint. |

## Rules while you do this

- **Privacy.** Record areas, never addresses. The tracker stays private.
  Nothing about a maker goes on social media until they have said yes and
  seen the post.
- **Anti-spam.** One-to-one messages to people whose details you found in
  public are fine; bulk email to a scraped list is not (Canada's Anti-Spam
  Legislation). The only email list is the launch list, with its own consent
  and single launch-day email (A-8). Do not start a newsletter now.
- **Food safety.** Never tell a maker they are allowed to sell, and never
  tell them they are not. Point to the public health unit's guidance and the
  food handler course and leave it with them. Most of what they cook is not a
  low-risk food under Ontario's rules, and that is their responsibility, as
  the business plan says.
- **Respect the traditions.** Write down the tradition and the dish names the
  maker uses. "Caribbean" alone is not an answer; ask.
- **Say what you have.** The site is a launch list today and a directory on
  Nov 26. Say so. Show the preview and the video for what the product will
  be.

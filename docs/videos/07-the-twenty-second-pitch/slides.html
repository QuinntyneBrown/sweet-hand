The pitch, in full

Hi, I'm building Sweet Hand, a free site where people in Toronto find Caribbean home cooks by dish, island and neighbourhood. It opens at the end of November, right before Christmas orders. It's free to list, you set your own menu, batches and days, and your home address never shows, only your area. The first hundred cooks get a free photo shoot of their dishes in my studio downtown. Can I put you on the list?

Seventy words. Twenty seconds.

It answers every question a cook is about to ask, before they ask it.

Say it out loud until it sounds like you

Line 1 · What it is

Three words do the work: free, find, neighbourhood

Line 2 · When

Christmas is the reason to say yes today. Black cake fruit is soaking; sorrel orders open in November.

Line 3 · The three worries

Free · your menu, batches and days · address never shows. Say all three, every time.

Line 4 · The gift

About 90 minutes in the studio. They bring the food. They keep the photos.

Line 5 · The ask

A yes-or-no question. Never "what do you think?"

After the yes, two questions

1 · "Which dishes should I write down?"

Their words for the dish and the tradition: Trini, Guyanese, Bajan, Jamaican, Haitian. Never just "Caribbean".

2 · "Who else cooks that I should talk to?"

Then: "Can I say you sent me?"

Warm intro ≈ 1 in 2 · cold message ≈ 1 in 7

What you never say

  • That Sweet Hand certifies, inspects or approves a kitchen
  • That a listing is a licence, or anything like one
  • That orders are guaranteed

Say what exists today: a launch list now, a directory on November 26. Use the word "will".

Door 1 · People you know

Don't pitch. Ask about buying.

  • "Who makes your black cake?"
  • "Who did the food at the last christening?"
  • "Who do you order roti from?"

Expect 10 to 20 names this weekend.

Then one line to the cook

"Your cousin gave me your number and said you make the best pelau in Ajax. I'm building a free site so people find cooks like you by dish and neighbourhood. Can I put you on the list?"

Ask for three names, not two.

Door 2 · Cooks already posting

  1. Their dish, by name. "Your oxtail looks serious." So they know you looked.
  2. The one-liner and the gift. Free directory of Caribbean home cooks in the GTA, opening before Christmas. Free to list, address stays private, first 100 get a free studio shoot.
  3. The move. "Five minutes on the phone this week, or can I send details on WhatsApp?"

Never close inside Instagram. 20 to 30 new DMs a day; reply within the hour.

Door 3 · The shop counter

You're pitching the person who knows the cooks.

"I'm building a free directory of Caribbean home cooks. Do you know people who sell from home?"

Same block: the salon and the barbershop

Where cooks get recommended. The owners often sell food on the side themselves.

Door 4 · Church

  1. Call the office first. Ask for the pastor or the hospitality committee: two minutes at the announcements, or a table after the service.
  2. Start with the kitchen. The people who run the church kitchen know every cook in the congregation. Ask them to walk you to the cooks.
  3. Harvest and bazaar season. Every table at a bake sale is a maker.

Asking for help with something that helps their own people. Not selling at church.

Things to remember

  • Five lines: what it is, when, the three worries, the gift, the ask
  • After every yes: which dishes, and who else
  • Never certified, licensed or guaranteed; say what exists today
  • Known: ask about buying first · Instagram: three lines, then a call · Shop: pitch the owner · Church: start with the kitchen

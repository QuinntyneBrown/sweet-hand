# 08 · Handling objections

Every door has its own no, and most of them aren't a no. They're a worry. This video goes through the eight you'll hear most, where you'll hear each one, and what to say back. The second voice is the cook.

## I don't have a licence

You'll hear this one at the shop counter and at church more than anywhere, because those are the cooks who sell quietly to people they trust.

**Maker:** I don't have a licence. I can't be on a website.

The site never says anyone is certified. You write your own food safety note, in your words, or you write nothing. It's your call, and your page says exactly what you choose.

Then stop. Don't argue, and don't reassure. Here is the rule for the whole campaign: never tell a cook they're allowed to sell, and never tell them they're not. Most of what they cook is not a low-risk food under Ontario's rules, and that is their responsibility, not yours. Keep two links ready on your phone, the public health unit's home food guidance and the food handler course, send them when asked, and leave it with them.

## My address

You'll hear this everywhere, and it's the easiest one, because the product was built around it.

**Maker:** I don't want my address out there. I don't want strangers at my door.

It never shows. You pick a neighbourhood or an intersection, and that's all anyone sees. Pickup details go only to the customer you choose, when you choose.

This is the line people repeat to their friends, so say it at every door, whether or not they ask.

## I only cook for people I know

People you know and church cooks say this. It isn't a no. It's a cook telling you they're small, and small is the point.

**Maker:** I only cook for people I know. I'm not a restaurant.

That's fine. You set the batches and the days. A page that says "by pre-order, Fridays only, twenty plates" is exactly what the site is for. Nobody turns you into a restaurant.

Then ask for the dishes. Once they're describing the pelau, they're in.

## What does it cost

Everyone asks this, and shop owners ask their own version: what's in it for me?

**Maker:** So what's the catch? What does it cost?

Nothing. Listing and searching stay free. Later there's an optional paid tier for cooks who want extras, and nobody has to take it.

For the shop owner: the cooks are your best customers. A flyer at the till costs you nothing, and when they get found, they buy more from you.

Don't oversell the paid tier and don't hide it. One sentence, then move on.

## Who are you

Instagram and Facebook cooks ask this, because strangers message them all day.

**Maker:** Who are you, though? I've never heard of Sweet Hand.

Fair. I'm a photographer and an engineer, and this is my own work. Here's the site, and here's a ninety-second video of what it does.

Show the preview on your phone and the "Cater with Sweet Hand" video. Say what's true: a launch list today, a directory on November twenty-sixth. And say "will", not "is", for everything that isn't built yet.

## Send me something

This is the Instagram and WhatsApp version of maybe.

**Maker:** Just send me something and I'll look at it.

Sure, sending it now. I'll check back with you Thursday.

Send the WhatsApp image and the link, and put a date on the follow-up out loud. "Send me something" is a maybe, not a yes, so it goes in the tracker as replied, not as yes. Follow up in forty-eight hours, and if the second message gets nothing, call.

## Not at church

Sometimes the church office says no before you reach a single cook.

**Maker:** We don't do business announcements during the service.

Understood. Could I speak to the hospitality committee, or the people who run the kitchen? And could I take a table after the service, or help at the bazaar?

The announcement was never the point. The kitchen committee is. If the answer is still no, ask one person from that congregation in your own network to make the introduction instead.

## I'm too busy

You'll hear this from the busiest cooks, which is to say the best ones, especially with Diwali on November eighth and Christmas orders opening.

**Maker:** It's the season. I'm swamped. Come back in January.

The list takes one minute, right now, on your phone. The photo shoot is ninety minutes whenever you choose. And the site opens before Christmas, so being on it is for this season, not next year.

Then do the minute with them. Open sweethand.ca, press "Join as a caterer", and watch them type. Don't leave it for later.

## Things to remember

A worry is not a no. Answer it in one or two sentences, then ask for the dishes.

Never say allowed, never say not allowed. Send the links and leave it with them.

The address line is the one people repeat. Say it at every door.

"Send me something" is a maybe. Put a date on the follow-up.

If a cook has one minute, use it on the list, not on the pitch.

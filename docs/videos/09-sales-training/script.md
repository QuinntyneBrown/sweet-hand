# 09 · Sales training: signing up caterers

This video is for Sweet Hand sales people. In about five minutes you'll learn who to approach, how to run the pitch, what to say to the usual objections, how to help a caterer register on the spot, and how to get the Facebook follow. Every conversation ends with two asks: register on Sweet Hand, and follow the Sweet Hand Facebook Page.

## The kit

Everything you need is in one folder, the caterer sales kit. The elevator pitch has a thirty-second version, a sixty-second version and a message you can paste into WhatsApp. The FAQ answers the questions caterers ask, written so you can read it out. There's a slide deck with speaker notes for a laptop or a tablet, a one-page leave-behind, a half-page photoshoot card, an image to share on phones, and a short video for caterers. Learn the pitch, and keep the FAQ open on your phone.

## Who to approach

Sweet Hand's first release is for caterers: people who cook Caribbean food at home or at a small scale in Toronto or the GTA, and take orders for events. Not restaurants or food trucks, not yet. Ask which tradition they cook. Jamaican, Trinidadian and Tobagonian, Guyanese, Bajan, Haitian, Grenadian, Vincentian, Lucian: write down their words, never just Caribbean.

Find them where they already sell: Instagram, Facebook groups, WhatsApp, Caribbean grocers, churches, and the caterers you already know. Every caterer who says yes knows two more, so always ask for names.

## Running the pitch

Start with how people find them now: a cousin, a church group, a WhatsApp chat. Then say what Sweet Hand is: a site where people in Toronto and the GTA find Caribbean caterers by dish, by island and by area, opening on Thursday the twenty-sixth of November. Then the three promises we can keep. It's free to list. They set the menu, the batches and the days. Their home address never shows.

Then the offer, word for word, every time. The first 100 caterers who register get a free photoshoot: 10 of their dishes, shot in our studio. In return we ask for a complete profile and a launch-day post. How the first hundred are counted and the last day to book are still to be confirmed by the founder, so don't promise anyone a place. Say: register today, and I'll confirm your place.

## The objections

"I already sell on Instagram." Keep it. Sweet Hand brings people who don't follow them yet, and the photos from the shoot are theirs to post. "I don't have time." Signing up takes two minutes, and you'll do it with them now. "What does it cost?" Nothing. Listing is free, with no fees. "Will people see my address?" No. Their page shows the areas they serve, and their phone and email stay private until they choose to share them. "Do you deliver?" Not at launch. They agree pickup or delivery with each customer. Delivery through a courier partner is something we're exploring, not a promise.

## Registering on the spot

Do it with them, on their phone, while you're there. Go to sweethand.ca and press Join as a caterer. Before launch day, that puts them on the launch list as a caterer: a business name and an email, then they confirm the link we send. From the twenty-sixth of November, the same button opens caterer registration: business name, their own name, email, phone and a password, two boxes to tick, then Create caterer account.

After that they build their page as they go. It saves by itself and works on a phone. Here a caterer adds a dish, ticks its dietary tags and adds a price. To send the page in they need photos, at least three dishes and a current food handler certificate, and our team reviews every page within three business days.

## The Facebook follow

Then the second ask, before you put your phone away. Open Facebook with them, scan the code on the flyer or the deck, and follow the Sweet Hand Page. Tell them why: that's where we announce the launch, the photoshoot days and the caterers who join.

## What never to promise

Never say Sweet Hand certifies, inspects or approves a kitchen, or that a listing is a licence. Never promise orders. Delivery and awards, like GTA's Best Jerk Chicken, are ideas we're exploring: say exploring, every time. Don't promise a photoshoot place, or a date for anything except the launch. And never write down anyone's home address.

## Log the lead

Log every conversation the same day in the founder's tracker: their name and business, how to reach them, their tradition, dishes and area, the status, whether they followed on Facebook, and who else they told you about. Areas only, never addresses.

## Things to remember

Two asks, every time: register, and follow. Say the photoshoot offer word for word. Delivery and awards are ideas we're exploring. Sweet Hand never certifies a kitchen. And log the lead the same day. Now go and find someone with a sweet hand.

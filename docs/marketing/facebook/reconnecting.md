# Reconnecting on Facebook

Messages for people you have not talked to in years who might cook and sell,
know someone who does, or be useful to Sweet Hand some other way. They are
sent one at a time from your own Facebook account, not the Page. They make the
offer in [the maker recruitment plan](../../plans/maker-recruitment.md#the-pitch)
and nothing more: free to list, the address never shows, opening the end of
November, and a free photo shoot for the first 100 makers.

Fill in the **[brackets]** and make the first line about them. That one line
is what makes it read like you, not a mass message.

## Messages by scenario

### 1. You think they sell food

You have seen their posts or heard they sell.

> Hey [name]! It's been years, hope you and the family are doing well. I keep
> seeing your [dish] on my feed and it looks serious 👀
>
> Random question: are you still selling? I'm building something called Sweet
> Hand, a free site where people in Toronto and the GTA can find Caribbean home
> cooks by dish, island and neighbourhood. It opens at the end of November,
> just before Christmas orders.
>
> It's free to list, you set your own menu, days and batch sizes, and your home
> address never shows, only your area. The first 100 makers also get a free
> photo shoot of their dishes.
>
> Would you be open to me adding you, or to a quick 5-minute call? No pressure
> at all.

### 2. They are well connected but do not cook

Church, community groups, events: they know everybody.

> Hi [name]! Long time. I think the last time we properly talked was
> [memory/place]. Hope life's been good to you.
>
> I'm reaching out because you've always known everybody 😄 I'm building Sweet
> Hand, a free site to help Caribbean home cooks and small-batch makers in the
> GTA get found: the aunties doing roti on Fridays, the black cake and sorrel
> people, the patty and pepper sauce makers.
>
> Do you know anyone who cooks and sells from home who might want more
> customers? Even one or two names would help me a lot. Happy to tell you more
> if you're curious.

### 3. Someone in their family cooks

A mother, aunt, cousin or grandparent whose cooking you remember.

> Hey [name]! It's been forever, hope you're well. I still remember your
> [mom's/aunt's] [dish] from back in the day, honestly some of the best I've
> ever had.
>
> Do they still cook? I'm building Sweet Hand, a free site where people in
> Toronto can find Caribbean home cooks by dish and neighbourhood. It opens at
> the end of November. It's free to list, they set their own days and amounts,
> and their address never shows, only the general area.
>
> If they'd be interested, I'd love to talk to them, or I can send you the
> details to pass along. The first 100 cooks also get a free photo shoot of
> their food.

### 4. They work near food

A caterer, event planner, promoter or shop owner.

> Hi [name]! Long time no talk. I've been seeing your work with [their
> business/events] and it looks like it's really taking off. Congrats!
>
> I'm building Sweet Hand, a free directory of Caribbean home cooks and
> small-batch food makers across Toronto and the GTA, launching at the end of
> November. Since you're in the [food/events] world, I figured you'd either
> know people who should be on it, or have ideas about what makers actually
> need.
>
> Would you be up for a quick call sometime this week or next? I'd really value
> your take.

### 5. A light reconnect first

Use this when it has been so long that pitching straight away would feel too
forward. Bring up Sweet Hand once they reply.

> Hey [name]! You just popped into my head and I realized it's been way too
> long. How have you been? What are you up to these days?

After they reply:

> Love that! On my end, I've been building something I'm really excited about.
> It's called Sweet Hand, a free site to help Caribbean home cooks in the GTA
> get found by people looking for their food. It opens at the end of November.
> You came to mind because [reason: you cook / you know everybody / your
> family's cooking]. Could I tell you a bit more?

### 6. Follow-up when there is no reply

Send after 3 to 5 days, once.

> Hey [name], just floating this back up in case it got buried. No pressure at
> all! Even a "not for me" is totally fine. And if you know anyone who cooks
> and sells from home, I'd really appreciate a name 🙏

## Someone who once shared their cooking and knows a scene

The strongest contact is often both a cook and a connector: an old co-worker,
classmate or church friend who used to bring you food, and who is now deep in
a community scene such as Carnival (mas camps, fêtes, the vendors on the
Lakeshore), a church or a sports league. Ask them two things at once: would
they list themselves, and who else cooks.

### When the first message was too vague

A short "I wanted to chat to you about Caribbean food and catering, give me a
call" is the right ask, because a yes almost never happens over chat. But after
years apart they cannot tell why you are writing: it could mean you want to
hire them, sell them something, or something else. If there is no reply in one
or two days, send a follow-up that says what Sweet Hand is, without asking for
the call again:

> Hey [name], I should have said more in my first message 😄 I still remember
> you bringing me [dish] at [work/school/church] back in the day!
>
> I'm building Sweet Hand, a free site where people in Toronto and the GTA can
> find Caribbean home cooks and caterers by dish and neighbourhood. It opens at
> the end of November. You came to mind right away, both for your cooking and
> because you know everybody in the [Carnival/church/community] scene.
>
> Would love to catch up and tell you more whenever you have 10 minutes. No
> pressure at all!

If they have already replied, just call. You do not need this.

### If they reply and are open to it

> You have no idea how happy that makes me! That [dish] set the bar 😂
>
> Here's the idea: Sweet Hand opens at the end of November, just before
> Christmas orders, so people in the GTA can find Caribbean home cooks by dish
> and neighbourhood. You could list your [dish] (and whatever else you make)
> for the days that work for you. And if you know people from the [scene] who
> cook and sell, the vendors, the mas camp cooks, the aunties doing black cake,
> I'd love an introduction.
>
> Easier on WhatsApp or a quick call?

### If they say they do not sell

> Totally fair! I still think about that [dish] though 😄 Do you know anyone
> from the [scene] who cooks and sells from home? Even one name would help me
> a lot.

### What to ask a connector for, in order of value

1. **Direct introductions.** "Can I say you sent me?" makes a stranger far
   more likely to say yes.
2. **Their WhatsApp groups.** If they are in band, vendor or church groups,
   send them the maker image (`docs/marketing/whatsapp/status-2-makers.png`)
   with two lines of text so sharing is easy for them.
3. **Who to talk to.** Ask who the main food people are in that world: vendor
   coordinators, mas camp leads, fête promoters, church kitchen leads.

Carnival is off-season in the autumn, when many vendors are cooking for
Christmas orders, which is a good reason to contact them now.

## On the call

1. **Catch up first.** Ask about their life before you mention Sweet Hand.
2. **Give the 20-second pitch.** It's free to list, they set their own menu
   and days, their address never shows (only their area), it opens at the end
   of November, and the first 100 cooks get a free photo shoot.
3. **Ask whether they still cook or sell.** If yes: "Can I put you on the
   list? Which dishes should I write down?"
4. **Ask for names, whatever their answer.** "Who else cooks and sells? Can I
   say you sent me?"
5. **Ask about WhatsApp groups.** Offer to send the image and two lines of
   text to share.

Afterwards, write down their tradition (Trini, Guyanese, Jamaican and so on),
their dishes and any names they give you in the tracker the recruitment plan
describes. Message those people within 24 hours while the introduction still
carries weight.

## Rules for every message

- **Hold the link back at first.** Messages from someone you have not talked
  to in a while can land in "Message requests", and a link can make it look
  like spam. Send `sweethand.ca` once they reply.
- **Always ask for names.** Even a "no" can lead to someone else: "Who else
  cooks that I should talk to?"
- **Do not promise orders or customers.** "People will be able to find you" is
  true; "you'll get orders" might not be.
- **Never say Sweet Hand approves, inspects or certifies anyone's kitchen.** If
  they bring up licensing, they write their own food-safety note in their own
  words, or leave it out.
- **Move it off Messenger quickly.** A yes almost always happens on WhatsApp
  or a call: "Easier if I send you the details on WhatsApp?"
- **Write down the tradition and the dish names they use.** Ask "Trini?
  Guyanese? Jamaican?" rather than writing "Caribbean", and spell dishes the
  way they do. Trini curry and Jamaican curry are not the same thing.
- **Do not offer money or a role you cannot back up yet.** A warm thank-you,
  and crediting a connector when their people get listed, goes a long way.
- **Keep it private.** Phone numbers, names and anything personal stay in the
  tracker, never in this repository or on social media.

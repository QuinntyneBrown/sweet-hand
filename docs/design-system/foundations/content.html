Foundation

Content

Words in Sweet Hand are few, warm and exact. The food does the talking; the copy tells people where they are, what happens next and what is private.

Overview

The design is minimal text, lots of photos and generous white space, and the copy follows it. A caterer card is a name and one quiet line ("Trinidadian & Tobagonian · Scarborough"); a button is a verb; an error says what to do. Every string in the product lives in resource files (L2-064.4), so these rules apply to the files, not just the screens.

  • Short. Cut every word that does not help someone choose a caterer, send an enquiry or run their business.
  • Warm, not cute. Speak like a good host: friendly and direct. No exclamation marks in the interface, no puns about spice, no "Oops!".
  • Specific. Name the caterer, the dish, the date and the number: "Enquiry sent to Pelau House", not "Success".
  • Respectful. Name each island tradition as the caterer does, spell dishes as their communities do, and never expose more about a person than they chose to share.

Voice and tone

The voice stays the same everywhere: warm, plain, brief. The tone shifts with the moment.

MomentToneFrom the mocks
BrowsingOut of the way. Names, places and photos; almost no prose."60 caterers" · "Jamaican · Pickering" · "Serves 20–300 guests"
EnquiringReassuring and concrete about what happens next."Kendra gets an email now. We'll email you when she replies, and the conversation lives in your inbox."
Something went wrongCalm. Say what is safe, then what to do."Nothing you wrote is lost. Check your connection and try again."
Caterer setupEncouraging, practical, one step at a time."What do you cook best?" · "Add at least 3 dishes. A name is enough; descriptions, dietary tags and prices help people choose."
OperatorsNeutral and precise; consequences stated up front."This access is written to the audit log with your name, the time and your reason."
CelebratingQuietly pleased. A name and a fact, no exclamation marks."Thanks, Jean-Robert. Your review is live." · "Customers can find you now."
  • Address people as "you". Sweet Hand is "we" ("We'll email you when it's live").
  • Use contractions: "can't", "we'll", "it's", "didn't". They read as speech.
  • Use typographic apostrophes and quotes (’ “ ”), en dashes for ranges (20–300) and the ellipsis character (…).
  • One idea per sentence. Most sentences in the product are under 15 words.

Sentence case

Everything is in sentence case: page titles, headings, buttons, labels, menu items, tabs, table headers, toasts. Capitalize only the first word and proper nouns.

WriteNot
Send an enquirySend An Enquiry
Guests and occasionsGuests & Occasions
Food handler certificateFood Handler Certificate
Approve and publishAPPROVE AND PUBLISH

Proper nouns keep their capitals: Sweet Hand, Pelau House, Toronto Public Health, Scarborough, Jamaican, Haitian, Trinidadian & Tobagonian. Dish names are common nouns in running text and on menus ("Curry goat", "Macaroni pie"), capitalized only at the start of a menu line. Uppercase appears only through the .overline and .kind styles, never typed in caps.

Buttons and links

A button label is a verb, or a verb and its object, that says exactly what happens. A link label names where it goes.

Keep it openNo, CancelThe safe choice says what stays the same
Try againRetry, ReloadPlain words
Add your first dishGet startedNames the first concrete step
Browse caterersGo homeNames the destination by what's there
  • Use "Cancel" only to abandon an unsaved task in a dialog; it never deletes anything.
  • Destructive buttons name the action and its object: "Delete my account", "Suspend caterer", "Remove permanently".
  • Busy buttons keep their label and show a spinner (aria-busy="true"); the label never changes to "Saving…".
  • In repeated rows, add the object for screen readers: "Edit Curry goat", written as Edit<span class="visually-hidden"> Curry goat</span> or an aria-label.
  • Never "Click here", "Learn more" alone, or "Submit".

Prices are the caterer's own guidance, not quotes: "From $18 per person" or "$65 per cake". Sweet Hand never totals a price or adds tax; the caterer does that in the thread.

Respecting the traditions

The Caribbean is many islands and many kitchens. Use the specific tradition every time the caterer gives one.

  • Use the cuisine names from the list (L2-007): Jamaican, Trinidadian & Tobagonian, Guyanese, Barbadian (Bajan), Haitian, Grenadian, St. Lucian, Antiguan & Barbudan, Vincentian, Belizean, Surinamese, Kittitian & Nevisian, and so on. "Pan-Caribbean" only when the caterer chooses it.
  • Never write "Caribbean food" for a single caterer whose tradition is known. "Caribbean" is for Sweet Hand as a whole: "Caribbean caterers in Toronto and the GTA".
  • Spell dishes as the caterer and their community do, without italics, quotes or glossing: doubles, buss up shut, pholourie, aloo pie, pelau, callaloo, cou-cou, conkie, oil down, souse, griot and pikliz, cook-up rice, pepperpot, black cake, sorrel, mauby, saltfish buljol, paw paw chow.
  • Don't "correct" a caterer's spelling to a more familiar one, and don't translate dish names. Descriptions may explain ("Bara and curry channa with tamarind and cucumber chutney").
  • Mark phrases in another language with lang (Haitian Creole ht, Spanish es) so screen readers pronounce them properly.
Demerara KitchenGuyanese · Brampton Cou-CouBarbadian (Bajan) · North York
The card line is always the caterer's primary cuisine and primary area, never a generic "Caribbean".

Privacy copy

Caterers cook from home and customers share personal plans. Copy makes the boundaries visible at the moment they matter.

  • Areas, never addresses. A caterer is shown by the areas they serve ("Scarborough, East York, North York, Pickering, Ajax"), never a street, building or home address. Pickup and delivery details are arranged in the private thread.
  • Say who sees what, next to the field. A .private-note with the lock icon sits beside any private information:

Pelau House sees your name, not your email or phone.

Private until you share it in a thread

Only you and the Sweet Hand team see this. It never appears on your page.

Devon W. and Maracas Bay never see who reported this.

Real private notes from the enquiry, account, certificate and report screens.
  • Sharing contact details is always the person's explicit action ("Share my contact details"), and the result is stated: "Pelau House can now see your email and phone in this enquiry."
  • Reviews show first name and last initial ("Marcia T."). A deleted account shows "Former Sweet Hand user".
  • Seed and example content is fictional: .example email domains, 555 phone numbers, invented businesses.

Allergens, diets and food safety

Dietary and allergen information is structured data shown as tags, never buried in a description. Tags are words, so they read without colour or icons.

Sweet bread

  • Contains nuts

Paw paw chow

  • Vegan
  • Gluten-free
  • Spicy
Allergen tags (.tag--allergen) read "Contains …"; dietary tags name the diet.
KindLabelsRule
DietaryVegan, Vegetarian, Gluten-free, Dairy-free, Halal, SpicyAdjective, sentence case, hyphenated compounds
AllergenContains nuts, Contains shellfishAlways "Contains …"; never "Nut-free" unless the caterer states it as a dietary tag

The certificate is the caterer's statement, not ours. Sweet Hand records the food handler certificate a caterer provides (holder, issuer, number, expiry) and checks that it is present and in date; it does not inspect or certify kitchens. Never write "certified", "inspected", "approved kitchen" or "safe" about a caterer. Write "Food handler certificate valid until 14 Mar 2029" in the caterer's own dashboard, and attribute anything shown publicly to the caterer.

Error messages

Formula: what happened + what is safe + what to do. Lead with the outcome in the person's terms, reassure them about their work, then give one action. Never blame, never show codes alone, never "Oops".

Your enquiry wasn’t sent

Something went wrong on our side. Everything you typed is still here. Reference 7F3K-92QD.

What happened (title) · whose fault and what's safe · a reference for support. From dialogs/enquiry/failed.

Usage

Accessibility

  • Plain language. Short sentences and common words help everyone, including people reading in a second language and people with cognitive disabilities.
  • Alt text is the caterer's caption (L2-068.1): "Curry chicken scattered with shado beni over white rice". Captions are 5–125 characters and describe the food, not "photo of".
  • Link and button text stands alone (2.4.4): "View Pelau House", "Edit Curry goat". Avoid "click", "tap" and "see"; say "choose", "select" or "open" if a verb is needed at all.
  • Don't rely on shape, place or colour in instructions: not "the red button" or "on the right".
  • Error text is programmatically tied to its field with aria-describedby and announced on submit.
  • Abbreviations: spell out on first use where people might not know them (GTA is fine; "FH number" is not: write "Certificate number").

Tokens

Content has no tokens of its own. The type roles that carry it (--text-body, --text-label, --text-caption, --text-overline, --text-prose) and the reading measure (--layout-measure, 62 characters) are documented on Typography and Layout.

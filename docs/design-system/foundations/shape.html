Foundation

Shape

Round food photos and pill-shaped controls give Sweet Hand its soft, plated look. Five radii plus full, and two border widths, cover every corner and edge.

Overview

The reference design is built on circles: every caterer is a round photo, like a plate seen from above. The interface echoes it. Anything you press is a pill (buttons, chips, tags, the search field); containers have generous rounded corners; only tiny marks (a checkbox, a bar in a chart) are nearly square.

The rule: the radius grows with the element. A 20px tag is a pill, a 44px input has 12px corners, a 512px dialog has 20px corners and a phone bottom sheet 28px. Small radii on big panels look stiff; big radii on small boxes turn them into blobs.

The round photo

Every caterer, and every place a person or business is represented, is a round photo: .photo is a 1:1 box with --radius-full and overflow: hidden, and the image inside is cropped with object-fit: cover at a slight 1.03 scale so no edge pixels show. On hover the listing card scales the image to 1.08 inside the circle; the circle itself never moves.

Border widths

Two widths. Most edges are a 1px hairline; 2px is for marks that must read as a line rather than an outline.

Focus rings are not borders: they are a 2px outline with a 3px offset, owned by the Accessibility foundation, and follow the element's radius.

Where these came from. The base kit had literal 1px, 2px, 3px and 4px borders. 1px and 2px became the two tokens. The 4px sorrel stripe on the left of the error summary was dropped: the summary is now a tinted --radius-md box like an alert, so it needs no stripe. The 3px ring on the large spinner is still a literal in components.css and should move to --border-width-thick.

Usage

  • Pick the radius by size from the table above; do not choose by taste per component.
  • Pressable means pill. Buttons, chips, tags and the search field are always --radius-full. A rectangular button reads as an input.
  • Nested corners shrink. An element inside a rounded container uses a smaller radius than its parent (a md well inside a lg card), so the gap between curves stays even.
  • Photos are circles. Always 1:1, always --radius-full. The gallery and photo viewer are the only rectangular food photos.
  • Square against an edge. A sheet or banner that touches the screen edge has square corners on that side.
  • Hairline by default. Use thick only for marks that carry state (selected tab, ring status) or emphasis on hover.

Accessibility

  • Shape never carries meaning alone. Status labels pair a shape (dot, square, ring) with a word, so they work for people who cannot distinguish the colour or the shape (1.4.1, L2-069.5).
  • Edges hold 3:1. A pill control's outline uses --color-border-strong (3:1 on every surface, 1.4.11); radius does not lower that requirement.
  • Round targets are measured by their box. A 44px round icon button is a 44 × 44px target; never shrink the hit area to the visible circle.
  • Forced colours. Buttons, inputs, cards, chips, badges and tags get a 1px CanvasText border in forced-colours mode, so rounded controls keep a visible edge when backgrounds are removed.
  • Photos need alt text written by the caterer ("Chicken pelau in a steel pot with lime and cucumber chutney"); the circle crop must not hide what the alt text describes.

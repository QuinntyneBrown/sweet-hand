Pattern

Notifications

News that is not an answer to what someone just did: a reply arrived, email is running late, a certificate expired, maintenance is coming. Toasts for passing news, banners for conditions that last, inline messages for the thing in front of you, and the inbox as the only notification centre.

Overview

Notifications tell people something they did not just ask for, or something that outlasts the moment. Sweet Hand uses four channels and no others:

  • Toast (toast): passing news, in the corner, gone after 8 s unless it is an error: "New message from Pelau House", "Photo order saved", "Couldn’t suspend Flying Fish Kitchen".
  • Banner (alert and banner, .alert.banner): a condition that lasts across pages, full-bleed under the header: "Verify your email to send enquiries", "Your profile is suspended and hidden from customers", "Scheduled maintenance".
  • Inline (alert, inline message): a condition of the thing on screen, next to it: the fit warning on an enquiry, "Name change waiting for review" on Business details.
  • Email and badges: email for anything that matters when people are away (a reply, a decision, an approval); unread counts (badge) on Inbox, Enquiries and Queue. The inbox is the notification centre (open question 14); there is no bell.

Feedback on the person's own action (saved, sent, failed to send) follows the same visual components but is chosen by Feedback and loading.

Priority

  1. Danger site banner (incident): above everything, one at a time.
  2. Persistent account banner (verify or update email): one; if both apply, the bounce wins.
  3. Profile banner (dashboard only): one, for the current status.
  4. Inline alerts in the content.
  5. Toasts: at most three, danger first to stay.

Never show two banners of the same family. When a higher banner appears, a lower one waits for the next page load.

Stacking

  • At most three toasts; new ones join at the bottom.
  • When a fourth arrives, the oldest auto-dismissing toast leaves first. A danger toast is never pushed out.
  • Each toast keeps its own timer; hovering or focusing the region pauses all of them.

Responsive behaviour

  • Toasts: below 36rem they span the width 16px from each edge at the bottom, above the safe area and above the sticky enquiry bar and reply box; from 36rem they sit 24px from the bottom-right corner, 24rem wide.
  • Banners are full-bleed under the header, with the text centred in the container; actions wrap under the text on phones.
  • Banner and toast dismiss buttons grow to 44px below 48rem.
  • Toast text clamps at no width: titles are kept short so a toast stays three lines or fewer at 320px.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

The toast region is in the page from the start, empty, with role="status" and aria-live="polite", so a toast added later is announced without moving focus (WCAG 4.1.3). Danger toasts carry role="alert" and are announced at once. Banners use role="status", or role="alert" for incidents and suspensions; a banner that is already there on page load is read in order like any content.

Keyboard

KeyAction
TabBanner actions come right after the header; the toast region comes after main, so its actions are reachable without a pointer.
Enter / SpaceActivates a toast action or Dismiss. "Show message" scrolls to the message and moves focus there.
EscHas no effect on toasts (it belongs to any open dialog).

Focus

Notifications never take focus. Focusing anything inside a toast pauses its timer (WCAG 2.2.1); after Dismiss, focus moves to the next toast or back to main content.

Labelling

Dismiss buttons are named ("Dismiss", "Dismiss maintenance notice"). Badges carry the full phrase: aria-label="1 unread enquiry", "7 items waiting".

Tone is never colour alone: each tone has its own icon shape (circle-i, tick, triangle, circle-!) and the title says what happened.

Motion

Toasts slide up over 200 ms and fade out; reduced motion zeroes both. The timer line is decorative and hidden from assistive technology.

Touch

Below 48rem toast actions and banner and toast dismiss buttons grow to 44px (L2-065.1), with 16px between actions; from 48rem they use their compact size. Toasts never cover the sticky enquiry bar.

Content

  • Toast title: what happened, past tense, object named: "Photo added", "Reply sent", "Auntie Bev’s Kitchen is published", "Couldn’t suspend Flying Fish Kitchen".
  • Toast text: the consequence or what to do, one sentence: "Your page shows the new order within a minute." "Check your connection, then try again. Your message is still in the box."
  • Banner title: the condition: "You’re offline", "Verify your email to send enquiries", "Your profile is in review." Body: what it means or what to do, with exact dates ("Sunday 18 October, 1:00–3:00 a.m.").
  • Actions: one verb, two at most: "Show message", "View page", "Undo", "Try again", "Stay signed in", "Resend verification email". Never "Retry" (see the microcopy library).
  • Quote the reason when a person wrote it: "Changes requested: “Photos 2 and 5 look like stock images. Please upload photos of your own food.”"

Do and don't

Sources

  • Toast: info, success, warning, danger, with-action, stacked
  • Dashboard toast: info, success, warning, danger, with-action, stacked
  • Operator toast: info, success, warning, danger, stacked
  • Site banner: info, success, warning, danger, persistent
  • Profile banner: info, success, warning, danger, persistent

Drift fixed during extraction: the three stacked mocks gave three eviction rules ("a fourth replaces the oldest"; "the oldest auto-dismissing toast leaves first"; "older info and success toasts leave first, the error stays"); the system uses the last two together. Warning toasts auto-dismissed in the customer and dashboard families but stayed in the operator family; the rule is now "a warning with an action stays, one without auto-dismisses". The customer toast mock names its stacked text "Pelau House will get the email soon" where the single toast says "as soon as our mail service catches up"; the longer, specific line is the standard. dashboard-toast/danger labels its action "Retry"; the library string is "Try again". Site-banner mocks use .alert--info, which components.css does not define; info is the default .alert tone, so the modifier is dropped.

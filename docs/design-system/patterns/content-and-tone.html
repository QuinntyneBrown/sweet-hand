Pattern

Content and tone

One voice, warm, plain and brief, carried through whole journeys: a customer enquiring, a customer reviewing, an operator moderating, an operator suspending a caterer. This page shows how the tone shifts from step to step, using the rules and strings in Foundations · Content.

Overview

The rules for words live in Foundations · Content: Canadian English, voice and tone, sentence case, button and link verbs, numbers, dates, times and money, respecting the traditions, privacy copy, allergens and food safety, the error and empty-state formulas, and the microcopy library. Use those strings; do not copy them here.

This page is about journeys. A person meets ten or more strings on the way through one task, written by different people at different times. The tone has to hold from the button that starts the task to the email that ends it:

  • The same words for the same thing all the way through. The button says "Send an enquiry", the dialog is titled "Send an enquiry", the submit says "Send enquiry", the confirmation says "Enquiry sent to Pelau House".
  • Name people and places. "Kendra gets an email now", "Suspend Pelau House?", "Devon W. is emailed your reason".
  • Say what happens next at every step, and who will see what.
  • Tone follows stakes. Browsing is nearly wordless; a permanent removal spells out every consequence.

Tone by moment

The voice never changes; the tone moves along four dials. Pick the setting from the moment, not from who is reading.

Structure

Anatomy of a high-stakes message

The suspension dialog carries the most weight of any screen, so every part has a job.

  1. Question with the name"Suspend Pelau House?" The person knows exactly what and whom.
  2. What happens to the thingTiming and visibility, then whether it can be undone.
  3. What happens to peopleWho is emailed and what they are told, with real counts.
  4. Who reads the reasonThe label says it is sent; the help asks for something the caterer can act on.
  5. The verb from the title"Suspend caterer", never "Confirm" or "Yes".

Responsive behaviour

  • Write for 320px first. Button labels stay at three words or fewer so they never wrap; a label that wraps at 360px is too long.
  • Short dates in tight places, full dates on detail screens: "Sat 21 Nov" in rows, chips and the enquiry summary on phones; "Saturday 21 November 2026" in the thread header and dialogs.
  • Do not hide meaning in truncation. Names clamp at two lines on cards and truncate in rows, but the full name stays in the link text; consequences in confirmations never truncate.
  • Long names wrap at display size on the caterer page (open question 25 asks whether to step the size down below 36rem); copy should not be shortened to fit.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

Pages are lang="en-CA"; phrases in Haitian Creole or Spanish carry their own lang. Status words, counts and reasons are text, never images or colour.

Keyboard

Copy does not change keyboard behaviour, but instructions never depend on a pointer: write "choose", "select" or "open", not "click" or "tap" (see Content · Accessibility).

Focus

Where focus lands, the text must make sense alone: an error summary's heading ("Fix 4 things to send your enquiry"), a success title ("Enquiry sent to Pelau House"), a dialog title ("Suspend Pelau House?").

Labelling

Repeated actions add their object for screen readers ("Remove Chicken pelau", "Cancel upload of IMG_2076.heic"); counts are phrases ("1 unread enquiry"); unread rows start with a hidden "Unread.". Link text names the destination ("View caterer", "Back to inbox").

Content

Rules that matter most across a flow

  • Keep the noun. An enquiry is an "enquiry" everywhere (never "request", "booking" or "order"); a caterer is a "caterer"; a customer is a "customer" in operator copy and "you" in their own.
  • Keep the verb. The title's verb returns on the button: "Decline Priya’s enquiry?" → "Decline enquiry"; "Remove … permanently?" → "Remove permanently".
  • Times are Toronto times and say so where it matters: "until 3:00 a.m. Toronto time", "Sunday 18 October, 1:00–3:00 a.m.". Money is the caterer's guidance in CAD: "From $18 per person", "about $2,600 before tax" (in the caterer's own message; Sweet Hand never totals). See formats.
  • Name the tradition. "Trinidadian & Tobagonian · Scarborough", never "Caribbean" for one caterer; dishes spelled as the caterer does: buss up shut, pholourie, shado beni. See traditions.
  • Privacy at the point of sharing: areas, never addresses; "Pelau House sees your name, not your email or phone."; "Devon W. and Maracas Bay never see who reported this." See privacy copy.
  • Food safety is the caterer's statement: "Food handler certificate valid until 14 Mar 2029", never "certified kitchen". Allergens read "Contains nuts". See allergens and food safety.

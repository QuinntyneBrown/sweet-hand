Queue item

A queue item is one thing waiting for an operator: a new profile, a name change, an expired certificate, a report or a held review. It shows what it is, who it is about and how long it has waited, and opens the record where the decision is made.

Overview

The operator console is built from two lists of queue items: the review queue (oldest first, everything that needs a decision) and the caterer records list (all 68 caterers, sorted by last change). Every row is one link to one record. The decision itself — approve, request changes, suspend — happens on the record page, never in the row.

  • Use a queue item when each row stands for a record an operator opens to act on, and the row's job is triage: what kind of thing, about whom, waiting how long.
  • Use an inbox row for conversations (customer and caterer enquiries): it adds unread weight and a time stamp instead of a wait.
  • Use list rows (.admin-row) when the row itself carries actions, such as cuisines and operators in settings.
  • Use a table when operators compare columns, such as the audit log with operator, action, reason and before → after.

Queue items are flat, like everything on Sweet Hand: a hairline between rows, no card, no shadow. Pepper red does not appear in a queue; the one "you are here" mark belongs to the current filter in the side navigation and segmented control above it.

Anatomy

  1. MediaThe caterer's round cover photo (.photo, 3.5rem) or, for things that are not a caterer, an icon in an .avatar (flag for reports, star for held reviews). Always decorative: alt="" or aria-hidden="true".
  2. Title.queue-item__title: the record's name, --text-body-lg medium. Wraps; never truncates.
  3. Wait or status.queue-item__wait: "Waiting 3 days" in tabular numbers, or a status label on record rows. Spans both text lines on the right.
  4. Kind.kind: what the item is ("New profile", "2 reports"), caption size, semibold, uppercase by CSS.
  5. Meta.queue-item__meta: cuisine · area, or the report reasons, separated by middots, --color-fg-muted.
  6. RowThe <a> itself: a three-column grid, --space-5 block padding, hairline --color-border-default below. The .queue list adds the hairline above the first row.

Variants

Review queue: waiting, late, and items that are not a caterer

Rows are sorted oldest first. When an item has waited 3 days or more — the "within 3 business days" Sweet Hand promises caterers — the wait turns .queue-item__wait--late: semibold in --color-warning-fg. Reports and held reviews are about a review or a complaint, not a caterer's food, so they show an icon instead of a photo.

Sizes

Queue items have one density: 3.5rem media, --space-5 block padding and a body-large title. Operators scan dozens of rows on a laptop, and the same rows must stay comfortable to tap on a phone, so there is no compact size. For denser data use a table.

States

A queue item is a link, so its states are a link's: default, hover (the row fills with --color-bg-surface-sunken), focus (the shared ring around the whole row) and loading. There is no disabled, selected or pressed row: an item an operator cannot act on is not in their queue. Forced here with data-state.

  • Loading keeps the row grid and swaps each part for a skeleton (circle, title, wait, meta) so nothing jumps when data arrives. The skeleton rows are aria-hidden="true"; a visually hidden role="status" says "Loading the review queue" once.
  • Empty is not a row state: the whole list is replaced by the empty state "All caught up — New profiles, name changes, expired certificates, reports and held reviews appear here, oldest first."
  • No results on the records list (a search plus a status filter) shows the active filters as removable chips above an empty state that says why: "No suspended caterers match “Pelau”".
  • Long queues (34 waiting, 5,214 records) stay one list; the page subtitle carries the summary ("34 waiting · oldest first · 9 waiting over 3 days") and pagination follows the list.

Responsive behaviour

  • All widths: a three-column grid (media · text · wait). The text column is minmax(0, 1fr) and the title wraps with overflow-wrap: anywhere, so "Pelau House → Pelau House by Kendra" wraps at 320px instead of pushing the wait off-screen. The wait never wraps (white-space: nowrap).
  • Below sm (36rem), --stack rows: the grid drops to two columns, the media spans three rows and the status label moves under the meta, left-aligned. Use --stack whenever the right slot holds a status label; plain waits are short enough to stay on the right.
  • md (48rem): the operator side navigation is a stacked list above the content; rows keep the full width of the container with --layout-margin either side.
  • lg (62rem) and up: the side navigation sits beside the list in the work layout; rows fill the main column. Nothing about the row itself changes.
  • Narrowest phones (320px): the title column is about 8rem once the photo and "Waiting 2 days" take their share, so titles wrap to two or three lines. That is acceptable; a business name is never truncated. If operators find it cramped, apply the --stack layout to every row below 22.5rem (proposed rule).
  • xl (75rem): the container stops growing, so rows never get wide enough to lose the eye between title and wait.

Theming

Queue items read semantic tokens directly; there are no component tokens because the row has no variant colours. A theme changes --color-border-default, --color-bg-surface-sunken, --color-fg-muted and --color-warning-fg; the row picks them up.

In dark the hover fill is --palette-stone-900 on a --palette-stone-950 canvas — a quieter step than light, but hover is never the only cue (the cursor and the link target are). Late waits switch to the lighter mango step so they keep 4.5:1 on the dark canvas. Photos sit on --color-bg-photo until they load, in both themes.

/* A denser operator theme could tighten rows without touching .queue-item */
[data-density="compact"] .queue-item { padding-block: var(--space-3); }

Accessibility

Role and pattern

A list of links: <ul class="queue">, one <li> per item, one <a class="queue-item"> per row (APG Link). Screen readers announce "list, 7 items", which matches the subtitle. The row is not a grid or listbox: there is no selection, and arrow keys stay with the screen reader.

Keyboard

KeyAction
Tab / Shift+TabMoves row by row, oldest first. Each row is a single tab stop because it holds no other controls.
EnterOpens the record. Returning with the browser's Back puts focus back on the same row.

Focus

The shared ring (2px --color-focus-ring, 3px offset) outlines the whole row with --radius-sm corners. Because the offset sits outside the row, the hairlines above and below stay visible and the ring is never clipped by the list.

Labelling

The link's name is its text in DOM order: title, wait, kind, meta — "Auntie Bev’s Kitchen, Waiting 3 days, New profile · Jamaican · Oshawa". Keep that DOM order even though the grid places the wait on the right. Media is decorative (alt="", aria-hidden="true") because the title already names the caterer. Write the arrow in name changes as <span aria-hidden="true">→</span><span class="visually-hidden">renamed to</span>; a bare "→" is read as "right arrow".

Contrast

Late is not carried by colour: the number of days is the information, and the semibold weight plus the page subtitle ("9 waiting over 3 days") repeat it.

Motion

Only the hover fill animates, over --duration-fast with --ease-standard. Reduced motion zeroes the duration. Loading skeletons stop shimmering under reduced motion and stay as static blocks.

Touch

Each row is the full width of the list and at least 96px tall (56px media plus 2 × 20px padding), far above the 44px target. Below 48rem rows sit 8px apart (L2-065.1), each keeping its hairline; from 48rem they touch, and each is one target.

Content

  • Title is the thing's own name: the business name exactly as the caterer wrote it ("Auntie Bev’s Kitchen", "Breadfruit & Butter"); for name changes "Old → New"; for reports and held reviews "Review of Maracas Bay by Devon W." (reviewers are first name and initial, as on the public page).
  • Kind is a short noun phrase in sentence case — the CSS uppercases it: "New profile", "Resubmitted", "Name change", "Certificate expired", "2 reports", "1 report", "Held review". Count reports with a numeral.
  • Meta gives the specific tradition and the primary area — "Vincentian · Brampton", never "Caribbean · GTA" — or the report reasons as the reporter chose them ("Fake or spam · Inappropriate content"), or why a review was held ("contains a phone number").
  • Wait is relative and coarse: "Waiting 2 hours", "Waiting 1 day", "Waiting 9 days". Record rows say "updated today", "updated Thu 8 Oct" (dates as "Thu 8 Oct", the year only when it isn't 2026).
  • Never put a caterer's email, phone or home address in a row; the record page shows contact details to operators only.

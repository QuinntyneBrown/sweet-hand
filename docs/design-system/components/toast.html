Toast

A toast confirms what just happened, out of the way, on the dark inverse surface: "Reply sent", "Photo removed", "Auntie Bev’s Kitchen is published". Errors stay until someone deals with them.

Overview

A toast is a short message that appears in a fixed region at the bottom of the screen after an action or a background event, then leaves on its own. It never blocks the page and never holds the only copy of important information: the thing it reports is always visible somewhere else too (the sent reply in the thread, the "Processing" label on the photo tile, the "Published" status in the queue).

Use a toast for:

  • Confirmation of an action: "Reply sent", "Caption saved", "Auntie Bev’s Kitchen is published".
  • Reversible actions, with Undo for the life of the toast: "Photo removed · Undo".
  • Background events while someone is busy elsewhere: "New message from Pelau House", "New in the queue".
  • Failures of something just attempted that leave the page usable: "Your reply wasn’t sent · Try again", "Upload failed · Retry".

Use something else when:

  • The message describes a condition that lasts (email unverified, profile suspended): a banner or alert.
  • A form field is wrong: an inline message beside the field.
  • The result needs reading or a decision (an enquiry was sent and here is what happens next): the dialog's success state, see dialog.
  • The page or list failed to load: an empty state in place of the content.

All tones share one dark inverse surface. Tone is carried by the icon shape and the words, not by colour; this keeps every toast at full contrast in both themes and keeps pepper red free for "selected".

Anatomy

  1. Container--color-bg-inverse, --radius-md, padding --space-4, --shadow-3 (a toast floats, so it may lift).
  2. Tone icon20px, currentColor on the inverse surface; the shape (tick, triangle, circle-i, circle-!) carries the tone.
  3. Title.toast__title: what happened, label weight.
  4. Text (optional).toast__text: one sentence of detail, --text-body-sm at 90% opacity.
  5. Action (optional).toast__action inside .toast__actions: an underlined text button or link, at most one per toast.
  6. Dismiss.close: always present; 44px below 48rem, 24px from 48rem.
  7. Timer bar.toast__progress: 2px currentColor at 40% along the bottom edge, decorative and aria-hidden. Only on toasts that auto-dismiss.

Variants

Tones

Tone modifiers (.toast--info, --success, --warning, --danger) pick the icon and the live-region role; they add no colour. Danger is the only tone that changes behaviour: it never auto-dismisses and uses role="alert".

With an action

One action at most: Undo, Retry or Try again, or a link that goes to the thing reported ("Show message", "View your page", "Open report", "Download CSV"). The action is a <button> when it does something and an <a href> when it goes somewhere.

Stacked

At most three toasts show at once, 12px apart (--space-3), newest at the bottom, nearest the thumb. A fourth replaces the oldest auto-dismissing toast; a danger toast is never pushed out. Each toast keeps its own timer.

Region placement

ClassWhereUse
.toast-regionFixed, bottom; full width minus 16px below 36rem, 24rem at the bottom right from 36rem; above the safe-area inset.Every screen by default.
.toast-region--topFixed, top.Screens whose bottom edge is taken by a persistent bar, such as the sticky enquiry bar on a caterer page below 62rem, so a toast never covers "Send an enquiry". No mock uses it yet.
.toast-region--staticIn the flow, pointer events on.Documentation and mocks only.

Sizes

Toasts have one size: 24rem wide from 36rem, the full region width below it, height from content. A long title wraps; it is never truncated. The dismiss button and the action are 44px tall below 48rem and compact (24px) from 48rem (see Responsive).

States

The toast itself is not interactive; its action and dismiss button are. Hover and focus are forced with data-state; on the inverse surface the focus ring switches to --color-fg-inverse so it shows against the dark (light-theme) or pale (dark-theme) toast.

Lifecycle and timing

StateWhat happens
EnteringSlides up 16px and fades in over --duration-base with --ease-enter (slide-up).
ShowingInfo, success and warning auto-dismiss after 8 seconds (the minimum; a toast with a long caption may get longer, never shorter). The timer bar shrinks over that time.
PausedThe timer stops while the pointer is over the toast, while anything in it has focus, and while the window is hidden; it restarts from where it stopped. The timer bar freezes.
PersistentDanger toasts and time-critical warnings ("Your session ends in 5 minutes") never auto-dismiss and have no timer bar. They go when dismissed, when the action succeeds, or when the situation ends (the person stays signed in).
Leaving (data-state="leaving")Fades out and drops 8px over --duration-base with --ease-exit, then is removed from the DOM. Not shown statically here because it ends invisible.
ReplacedWhen a fourth toast arrives, the oldest auto-dismissing toast leaves first; danger toasts stay.

Responsive behaviour

  • Below 36rem (xs): the region spans the screen with a 16px gutter (left/right: --space-4) and sits 16px above the bottom plus env(safe-area-inset-bottom), clear of the home indicator.
  • Below 48rem (xs, sm): the dismiss button is 44px square (--target-comfortable) and pulls into the corner with a negative margin so the text column keeps its width, and .toast__action is at least 44px tall (L2-065.1). Compact sizes apply only from 48rem.
  • From 36rem (sm): the region is 24rem wide at the bottom right, 24px from both edges.
  • Caterer page below 62rem: use .toast-region--top so toasts never cover the sticky enquiry bar; from 62rem the enquiry panel is in the sidebar and the default bottom region is fine.
  • The region never covers a focused element (WCAG 2.4.11): toasts are short and sit in a corner; when a persistent toast is open and focus moves to something underneath it, the page scrolls that element clear.

Theming

The toast reads --color-bg-inverse, --color-fg-inverse and --shadow-3. Icons, text, action and timer bar all use currentColor, so a theme only has to keep the inverse pair at 4.5:1. The tone modifiers set --toast-tone to the text colour; it exists so a future theme could tint the icon, provided every tone still reaches 3:1 on the inverse surface in both themes (the mock kit's tinted icons did not, which is why they were removed).

In dark the inverse flips: the toast becomes pale stone (--palette-stone-50) with near-black text, so it still stands apart from the dark page. The shadow deepens (--shadow-3 uses higher opacity in dark) to keep the lift visible.

/* A campaign re-skin keeps contrast by changing the pair, not the parts */
[data-theme="carnival"] .toast { background: var(--color-action); color: var(--color-fg-on-action); }

Accessibility

Role and pattern

Live regions, following the APG Alert pattern and WCAG 4.1.3 Status Messages. The .toast-region is rendered empty with the page as role="status" aria-live="polite"; info, success and warning toasts are inserted into it and announced politely. A danger toast carries role="alert" (assertive). Because a live region nested in another can be read twice, test with NVDA and VoiceOver; if a danger toast is announced twice, render danger toasts into a sibling role="alert" container placed in the same stack.

Keyboard

KeyAction
Tab / Shift+TabToasts sit at the end of the DOM (after <footer>), so they are reached last; within a toast, the action comes before the dismiss button.
Enter, SpaceActivates the action or dismisses.
EscWhile focus is inside a toast, dismisses it and returns focus to where it was before. Does nothing otherwise, so it never fights a dialog.

Focus

A toast never takes focus when it appears. "Show message" scrolls to the new message and moves focus there; Undo restores the photo and moves focus to its tile; after a toast is dismissed with focus inside, focus returns to the element that had it before. Focused, the toast's timer pauses. The ring is --color-fg-inverse on the inverse surface.

Labelling

The announced text is the title followed by the text; keep the title meaningful on its own ("Upload failed", not "Oops"). Icons are aria-hidden. The dismiss button is named "Dismiss"; the action names its result ("Undo", "Retry", "Show message"). Never put essential information only in a toast: the thread shows the sent reply, the tile shows "Processing".

Contrast

Toast text, icon and focus ring on inverse surface
Toast divider (decorative, exempt)

Secondary text renders at 90% opacity, which keeps it well above 4.5:1 because the base pair is over 15:1 in both themes. The timer bar is decorative (40% currentColor) and carries no information that is not also announced.

Motion

Entry and exit read --duration-base, which reduced motion zeroes, so toasts appear and disappear instantly. The timer bar's shrink is a width change that stops under reduced motion; the timer still runs.

Timing

WCAG 2.2.1: auto-dismiss is at least 8 seconds, pauses on hover and focus, and nothing that cannot be found elsewhere disappears on a timer. Undo is a convenience on top of the confirmation dialog the person already answered ("Remove photo?"), so missing it loses nothing they did not choose.

Touch

Below 48rem the dismiss button is 44px square and the action at least 44px tall; from 48rem both are compact (24px minimum, WCAG 2.5.8) with 16px between action and dismiss. A toast cannot be swiped away; swipe would be the only way to dismiss with one gesture, and the button already does that (WCAG 2.5.7).

Content

  • Title: the result, in the past tense, in four words or fewer: "Reply sent", "Caption saved", "Photo removed", "Contact details shared".
  • Name the thing when there could be doubt: "Couldn’t suspend Flying Fish Kitchen", "Auntie Bev’s Kitchen is published".
  • Text: one sentence of consequence: "Pelau House will get an email.", "It appears in the listing within a minute."
  • Failures say what didn’t happen and what is safe: "Nothing was changed and the caterer wasn’t emailed.", "Your message is still in the box."
  • Quote file names and captions exactly, in curly quotes for captions: "IMG_2077.heic didn’t upload."; "“Doubles with tamarind and cucumber chutney” is now on your page."
  • Actions: one or two words, a verb: "Undo", "Retry", "Try again", "Show message", "View your page". No "OK" or "Got it"; the dismiss button does that.
  • No Undo for things that cannot be undone ("Reply sent": a sent reply cannot be unsent; an approval is reversed by suspending, not from a toast).

Divider

A divider is a 1px line between groups. Sweet Hand uses white space first and hairline row separators second; a standalone divider is the rare third choice.

WCAG 2.2 AAStatus: stable, rarely usedNative <hr>.divider

Overview

Sweet Hand screens are flat and airy: no card borders, generous gaps, and sections separated by space (--layout-section-gap, 64px on phones, 96px from 62rem). Lines appear in two ways:

  • Row separators, built into components: a 1px --color-border-default rule under every dish, settings row, inbox row, queue item, table row and key-value pair, a rule above each chart card, and the header and footer rules of a sheet and the sticky enquiry bar. These are borders on the component; they are not divider elements.
  • Dividers, for when two groups sit inside one container and space alone does not separate them: the line under the name and email in the account menu (.menu__divider), or a thematic break inside long prose.

Variants: horizontal (<hr> or .divider), vertical (.divider--vertical) and labelled (.divider--label). Don't use a divider between page sections (use space), between list rows (use the row component) or between inline metadata such as "Trinidadian & Tobagonian · Guyanese" (use the middle dot, as the caterer page does).

Anatomy

  1. Rule1px (--border-width-hairline) top border in --color-border-default; no background, no shadow.
  2. Spacing--space-6 above and below by default; the menu divider uses --space-1.
  3. Label (optional).divider--label: centred --text-body-sm in --color-fg-muted, with a rule on each side, --space-3 gap.

Responsive behaviour

  • All breakpoints: a horizontal divider spans its container; its spacing does not change.
  • XS (below 36rem): a vertical divider in a row that wraps would end up dangling at a line end; drop it below 36rem or use a horizontal gap instead. Labels in a labelled divider must fit on one line at 320px (two or three words).
  • Row separators follow their component: the dish list loses its top rule from 48rem when it becomes two columns, and the key-value list moves the rule under both term and value from 36rem.

Theming

Every divider and row separator reads --color-border-default (stone-200 in light, stone-750 in dark) at --border-width-hairline; the label reads --color-fg-muted. A stronger rule (--color-border-strong) is reserved for control edges, so a divider never looks like an input. On inverse surfaces (toasts) use --color-border-inverse.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

<hr> has the implicit separator role and marks a thematic break; use it only when the break means something. A purely visual line (a vertical rule, the menu divider) gets aria-hidden="true" or is a plain <div> border. A labelled divider uses role="separator" with an aria-label equal to its text, or, when it starts a group, should be a real heading instead (WAI-ARIA separator).

Keyboard

KeyAction
NoneDividers are not focusable. (A focusable separator is a splitter, which Sweet Hand does not use.)

Focus

Not focusable. Rules sit --space-6 from neighbouring controls, so focus rings (3px offset) never touch them.

Labelling

If people need the label to understand the grouping, a heading is better than a labelled divider: headings appear in the screen-reader headings list; separators do not.

Contrast

The rule is decorative: the grouping is also carried by space and order, so the 3:1 non-text contrast minimum does not apply (WCAG 1.4.11). The label is text:

Motion

None.

Touch

Not a target. A vertical divider between two buttons does not count as spacing: keep at least 8px between the buttons themselves.

Content

  • Divider labels are two or three words in sentence case: "Closed enquiries", "Earlier".
  • Don't use a divider to separate inline facts; use the middle dot with spaces: "Sat 21 Nov · 120 guests · Family gatherings".
  • Don't put "or" in a divider between two equal paths; Sweet Hand shows one primary path and a quiet text link for the other ("Already have an account? Sign in").

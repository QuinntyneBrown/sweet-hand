Component · Containers and overlays

Photo

Every caterer is represented by their food, cropped to a circle. The round photo is the brand's second signature after the pepper-red filter, and it has rules: one crop, a neutral placeholder, and alt text that describes the dish.

Overview

.photo is a 1:1 circle that crops whatever image it holds. It is the image inside the caterer card, the gallery and viewer, the occasion tiles, the caterer chip in dialogs ("Send an enquiry to Pelau House"), operator rows and review headers, and the trio of photos above sign-in and system pages (.photo-row).

  • Use it wherever food stands for a caterer or an occasion. The reference design shows food, never logos or storefronts.
  • People without a photo get an avatar with initials ("MT" for Marcia Thompson), not a grey photo circle.
  • Managing photos (reorder, caption, cover) uses photo tiles, which are square so the crop can be judged; the published page shows them round.

The image is scaled to 103% inside the circle so JPEG edges and tight crops never show a hairline at the rim. Parents that are links or buttons zoom it further on hover (1.08 in the caterer card and the gallery).

Anatomy

  1. Circleaspect-ratio: 1, --radius-full, overflow: hidden, --color-bg-photo behind the image so a slow load shows a calm disc, not a hole.
  2. Imageobject-fit: cover, scaled 1.03, with width and height attributes so the layout never shifts (L2-006.5).
  3. Placeholder iconThe plate icon (two circles) at 32px in --color-fg-subtle; a clock while processing.
  4. Label.photo__label: a caption-size word near the bottom ("Cover photo", "Processing", "Photo unavailable").

Variants

Loaded, placeholder (no photo yet), processing (just uploaded), failed, loading. At 96px the label is left out; it appears from the large size and in cards.

Photo row

.photo-row: three photos, the middle one larger (72 / 104 / 72px), above "Page not found", "Sign in" and "Someone with a sweet hand". Decorative, so alt="" and the row is hidden from assistive technology.

Sizes

By default a photo fills its container's width (cards cap it at --photo-card-size, 280px). Fixed sizes are for photos beside text.

xs 40px (review headers in "My reviews"), sm 56px (caterer chip in dialogs, operator wells), md 96px (operator caterer pages, suspend and remove dialogs), lg 160px (reserved; no mock uses it yet).

A photo is not interactive: hover, focus and press belong to the link or button around it (caterer card, gallery). Its own states are about the image.

Both themes

  • Loading uses a circular skeleton inside the photo (.photo > .skeleton fills it), so the final layout is in place before data arrives.
  • Failed keeps the circle and size, shows the plate icon and, where there is room, "Photo unavailable". The viewer adds "This photo didn't load." and "Try again".
  • Processing (after upload, before resizing finishes) shows a clock and "Processing"; it is replaced in place when the sizes are ready.

Responsive behaviour

  • Fluid photos follow their column: one 280px circle per row on phones, two from 36rem, three from 62rem in the listing.
  • Every listing photo declares srcset with 400, 800 and 1200 widths and sizes matching the grid, served as AVIF or WebP (L2-006.1, .2). The first six load eagerly; the rest use loading="lazy" (L2-006.3).
  • width and height attributes plus aspect-ratio: 1 reserve the circle before the image arrives (L2-006.5).
  • In the viewer the photo is min(100%, 62dvh, 34rem), so it fits a landscape phone without scrolling.

Theming

The photo reads --color-bg-photo (stone 100 in light, stone 800 in dark) behind the image, --color-fg-subtle for placeholder icons and --color-fg-muted for labels. Images are never filtered, dimmed or tinted in dark: food colour is the content. A theme changes only the placeholder disc.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

A plain <img> inside a presentational span. A photo with no image (placeholder or failed) that carries meaning on its own gets role="img" and a label ("Photo 4 of 6 didn't load"); inside a link or button, the parent's name says it instead. See W3C Images tutorial.

Keyboard

KeyAction
NonePhotos are never focusable. Wrap them in a link or button when they do something.

Focus

The parent's ring follows the circle: the gallery button keeps --radius-full on focus so the ring is round.

Labelling: alt text for food photos

  • The alt is the caption the caterer wrote for that photo (L2-006.4, L2-068.1): 5 to 125 characters describing the food.
  • Describe the dish, the way the caterer's community names it: "Curry chicken with rice and buss up shut in a steel karahi", "Curry chicken scattered with shado beni over white rice". Not "Photo of food", not "Image 2".
  • Don't repeat the caterer's name; the card or page already says it. Never fall back to "Food from Pelau House".
  • Decorative photos (the photo row on system and sign-in pages, occasion tiles where the name is the label) use alt="".

Contrast

Text over a photo (scrim)

--color-bg-photo resolves to the same stone step as --color-bg-subtle in both themes, so those pairs are the checked ones. Text over a photo always sits on --color-bg-scrim (L2-069.2).

Motion

Hover zoom in a parent transitions scale over --duration-slow (320ms). Under reduced motion the transition is removed (the zoom still happens, instantly).

Touch

Photos are not targets. When wrapped, the link or button is at least the photo's size; xs and sm photos are never the only target in a row.

Content

  • Caterers upload photos of their own food; stock images are grounds for "Request changes" ("Photos 2 and 5 look like stock images. Please upload photos of your own food.").
  • Centre the dish: the circle crops the corners, so steel pots, karahis and cake stands should sit in the middle third.
  • Labels are one or two words: "Cover photo", "Processing", "Photo unavailable".
  • Captions use Canadian English and the caterer's spelling ("buss up shut", "pholourie", "black cake").

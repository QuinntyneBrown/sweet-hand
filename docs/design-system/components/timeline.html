Timeline

A timeline lists what happened to a record, in order: submitted, changes requested, published, suspended. Operators read it on every caterer record and in every decision dialog; caterers see one after they submit, as the steps still to come.

Overview

A timeline is an ordered list with a 2px rule down its left edge. Each entry is a time stamp, what happened and, where it matters, who did it and why. It never has dots, icons or colour: the order and the words are the information.

  • Status history: on pages/admin-caterer and in the suspend, remove, reinstate, approve and request-changes dialogs, newest first, so the operator sees the latest decision and its reason before acting again.
  • History: a short record of events in sentence form, such as a name change request ("Name change requested by Kendra Baptiste").
  • Next steps: after a caterer submits their profile, what happens now, then within 3 business days, then on approval. Forward order, no time stamps.

Use the audit log table when operators need to filter and compare many events across caterers (operator, action, target, reason, before → after). Use message thread for conversations, and description list for a record's current values.

Anatomy

  1. RuleThe list's left border: --border-width-thick in --color-border-default, with --space-4 padding to the text.
  2. When.timeline__when on a <time datetime>: caption size, --color-fg-subtle, Toronto time.
  3. Event · actorThe event in <strong> (a status word where there is one), a middot, then the person.
  4. Reason (optional).text-muted.text-sm: the operator's reason in curly quotes, or a short note ("Approved", "Resubmitted").

Sizes

Timelines have one size: caption time stamps, body text events and small reasons, --space-4 between entries. In dialogs they keep the same size; the dialog scrolls.

States

Timelines are read-only and not interactive: no hover, focus or disabled. Their states are about the data.

  • Long histories show the latest 10 entries and end with the "Audit log" link in the section head; the timeline itself never paginates.
  • Read-only: entries can't be edited or deleted; say so once under the list ("History entries can’t be edited").

Responsive behaviour

  • The timeline is one column at every width; entries are stacked (when, event, reason), so nothing has to fit side by side at 320px.
  • Long reasons wrap under the event; the rule runs the full height of the list.
  • In dialogs that fill the screen below 48rem the timeline scrolls with the dialog body; from 48rem it keeps the dialog's measure.

Theming

Reads --color-border-default (rule), --color-fg-subtle (time), --color-fg-default (event) and --color-fg-muted (reason). No component tokens. In dark the rule is stone-750 and the time stone-400. Don't tint entries by status; if the status matters visually, the status history's event word is already the status, in bold.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

An <ol> — the order is the meaning — with one <li> per event and a <time datetime> for the stamp. Screen readers announce "list, 5 items". Put the order in the heading or an aria-label ("Status history, newest first") because newest-first contradicts the numbering a screen reader may read.

Keyboard

KeyAction
TabNever stops in the timeline; the "Audit log" link in the section head is the next stop.

Focus

None.

Labelling

Each entry is self-contained: time, event, actor, reason. Don't rely on position ("the one above") or on the rule. Reasons are quoted exactly as the operator wrote them, because the caterer received the same words by email.

Content

  • Time stamps: "Fri 14 Mar 2025, 11:20 a.m." — weekday, day, short month, the year only when it isn't 2026, a comma, then the time with "a.m."/"p.m.". Toronto time.
  • Status history: the status word in bold (the same words as the status label: Published, Pending review, Changes requested, Suspended, Removed, Draft), a middot, the person's full name. Then the reason: quoted if an operator wrote it, a short past-tense note otherwise ("Approved", "Resubmitted", "Submitted", "Account created").
  • Sentence-form history: "Name change requested by Kendra Baptiste", "Published by Leah Mohammed". Pick one form per timeline; don't mix.
  • Next steps: a short time phrase in bold ("Now", "Within 3 business days") and one sentence each.

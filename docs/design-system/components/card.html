Component · Containers and overlays

Card

Sweet Hand is flat: content sits on the canvas, separated by space and headings, not boxes. Cards, wells and notes exist for the few places where something genuinely needs a surface of its own.

Overview

The reference design has no card borders or shadows: caterers are round photos with two lines of text, the menu is a list, the dashboard is headings and rows. Before reaching for a surface, try space and a heading. When content does need one, pick by what it is:

  • Well (.well): a sunken, borderless panel for quoted or secondary content: the review you are responding to (dialogs/review-response), the reported review an operator is judging, the sign-in "Quick check".
  • Closed note (.closed-note): a centred sunken line that ends a thread when nobody can reply: "This enquiry is closed.", "Sugar Dumplin hasn't replied yet. We'll email you when they do."
  • Contact card (.contact-card): a bordered block for contact details shared inside an enquiry thread, so they stand apart from the messages.
  • Private note (.private-note): not a surface but a muted line with a lock or shield icon that says who can see something: "Pelau House sees your name, not your email or phone." It appears in more than 100 mocks.
  • Bordered exceptions: the enquiry panel on the caterer page at 62rem and wider (.enquiry-panel) and the food handler certificate in the operator console (.certificate). Both are records people act on, and both are the only boxed thing on their screen.
  • Card (.card and modifiers): the general bordered surface from the base kit. No Sweet Hand screen uses it today. Keep it for a self-contained summary that must read as one object among others, such as a pending change an operator approves.

Don't use a card for a listing item (use the borderless caterer card), a row of data (use list rows), or to group form fields (use headings and form layout). Overlays have their own surfaces: dialog, sheet, popover.

Anatomy

  1. Title.card__title in --text-h4. Use a real heading level in context.
  2. Header meta.card__header puts a status label or icon button at the end.
  3. BodyAny content; --space-4 below the header.
  4. Footer.card__footer: hairline divider, actions at the end, the one primary last.
  5. Surface--color-bg-surface, 1px --color-border-default, --radius-lg, --space-6 padding, no shadow.

Sizes

Surfaces take their width from the layout and have one padding each: card --space-6, well and closed note --space-4, contact card --space-4, enquiry panel --space-8. A denser card would invite boxes into lists, which the brand avoids.

States

Static surfaces have no states. Interactive and selected cards do; hover and focus are forced with data-state, selection uses .card--selected (or aria-selected inside a listbox).

  • Hover on an interactive card lifts it 1px with --shadow-2 and a stronger edge; it is the only shadow a resting surface ever gets.
  • Loading: wells and cards keep their size and hold skeleton lines (pages/admin-report/loading), so nothing shifts when data arrives.

Responsive behaviour

  • All surfaces are fluid: they fill their column and never set a minimum width wider than 288px (the content box at 320px).
  • Enquiry panel: below 62rem it is not shown; its facts appear in the page (.facts.until-lg) and the button becomes the sticky enquiry bar. From 62rem it is a 22rem column, bordered, sticky --space-8 from the top.
  • Certificate: a two-column grid of label and value; values wrap anywhere (certificate numbers, long issuer names).
  • Contact card stays left-aligned inside the centred system message on every width.

Theming

No component tokens. Cards read --color-bg-surface and --color-border-default; wells and notes read --color-bg-surface-sunken; selection reads --color-accent. In light the surface and canvas are both white, so a card is just its hairline. In dark the surface equals the canvas (stone 950) and the sunken surface is stone 900, so wells stay visible and the border still carries the card. A theme that wants softer cards changes --color-border-default; it never adds a shadow to a resting card.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

Surfaces are presentational; the content inside sets the semantics. Quoted content uses figure and blockquote; a group of form controls in a well is a fieldset with a legend; the enquiry panel is an aside named "Enquire"; the certificate is a dl. An interactive card follows the APG Link pattern with one real link stretched over the card.

Keyboard

KeyAction
TabReaches the interactive card's single link (or each control inside a static card). Never make the card element itself focusable.
EnterFollows the card's link.

Focus

An interactive card shows the shared ring around the whole card (:focus-within); the inner link's own ring is suppressed so there is one ring, not two. Put no second interactive element inside an interactive card.

Labelling

The contact card's strong names whose details they are. A private note's icon is hidden; the sentence says exactly who sees what. When a thread closes while open, announce the closed note through the thread's status region.

The card's hairline (--color-border-default) is decorative: the card's content, not its edge, carries meaning, so it is exempt from 1.4.11.

Motion

The interactive card's lift and shadow transition over --duration-fast; reduced motion removes the lift and keeps the shadow change.

Touch

The interactive card's whole area is the target. Buttons in a card footer keep their own sizes and 12px gap (--space-3).

Content

  • Closed notes are full sentences that say what happens next: "Sugar Dumplin hasn't replied yet. We'll email you when they do."; "This person has left Sweet Hand, so replies can't be delivered."
  • Private notes name the audience: "Only you and the Sweet Hand team see this. It never appears on your page." Never just "Private".
  • Contact cards hold only what was shared: name, email, phone. No labels like "Email:"; the formats speak for themselves.
  • Certificate dates use "14 Mar 2029"; the certificate is presented as the caterer's own record.

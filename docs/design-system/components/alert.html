Alert and banner

Alerts tell people about a condition that holds right now: a certificate about to expire, a profile in review, a sign-in that failed. A banner is the same message stretched across the page under the header.

Overview

An alert is a tinted block with a tone icon, a short title, one or two sentences and, at most, one or two actions. It stays on screen for as long as the condition is true. Two layouts share the same markup:

  • Inline alert (.alert) sits inside the content it is about: "Your certificate expires on Monday 9 November 2026" above the certificate form, "We couldn’t sign you in just now" above the sign-in fields, "This chart didn’t load" inside one chart card.
  • Banner (.alert.banner) spans the page directly under the site header and is about the whole account or the whole service: scheduled maintenance, an unverified email, a suspended profile. There is at most one banner on a screen.

Choose something else when the message is not a standing condition:

  • A result of an action that people only need to glimpse ("Reply sent", "Photo order saved") is a toast.
  • A problem with one field is an inline message under that field; several field errors get the form's error summary (see Forms).
  • When the content itself failed to load or there is nothing to show, use an empty state; when the whole page cannot be served, an error page.
  • A confirmation that must be answered before continuing is a dialog.

Tone colours (info blue-grey, success callaloo green, warning mango, danger sorrel) are the only colour an alert adds. Pepper red never appears in an alert: it means "selected", not "important".

Anatomy

  1. ContainerTone background and 1px tone border, --radius-md (square edges for a banner), padding --space-4, gap --space-3.
  2. Tone icon20px outline icon in --alert-icon, aria-hidden="true". One fixed glyph per tone: info, success, warning, danger.
  3. Title .alert__title: the condition in one line, label weight at --font-size-md.
  4. Text (optional)One or two sentences: why it matters and what happens next.
  5. Actions (optional).alert__actions: one or two links or link buttons in the alert's text colour.
  6. Dismiss (optional).close: 24px icon button, 44px in banners below 48rem. Only for messages that can safely go away.

Sizes

Alerts have one size. Padding, icon and type are fixed so every alert reads the same; the banner is a layout variant (full-bleed, centred), not a size. The only size change is the dismiss button, which grows from 24px to 44px in banners below 48rem (see Responsive).

States

The container has no interactive states; its actions and dismiss button do. Hover and focus are forced with data-state. Live-region roles are left off the matrix so this page does not announce twenty alerts.

  • Dismissed removes the alert from the DOM (no hidden copy left behind) and remembers the choice per device for the life of the condition. A maintenance notice comes back on the day of the maintenance.
  • Busy action: a "Resend verification email" or "Try again" button inside an alert uses the button's own aria-busy state; the alert does not change while it works. When the resend succeeds, a toast confirms it and the banner stays (the email is still unverified).
  • Resolved: when the condition ends while the page is open (email verified in another tab, incident over), the banner is replaced by a dismissible success banner on the next page load, not mid-reading.

Responsive behaviour

  • All sizes: an inline alert fills the width of its column and wraps; text never truncates. Actions wrap onto a new line under the text (flex-wrap).
  • Below 48rem (xs, sm): banner title, text and actions stack as wrapped lines. The banner and toast dismiss buttons grow to --target-comfortable (44px) and pull into the corner with a negative margin so the text does not shift (L2-065.1).
  • From 48rem (md): the banner body is a single centred row (.banner .alert__body is flex-direction: row) when it fits; the dismiss button is 24px with at least 24px of clear space around it.
  • From 62rem (lg): inline alerts in the dashboard and console sit in the main column (.work-main), never across the side navigation; the profile banner sits above both.
  • Banners are never sticky. They scroll away with the page, so they never cover a focused field (WCAG 2.4.11) and never stack with the sticky enquiry bar.

Theming

The alert reads four component tokens that each tone overrides: --alert-bg, --alert-fg, --alert-border and --alert-icon. They alias the status roles (--color-info-*, --color-success-*, --color-warning-*, --color-danger-*). The dismiss hover is an 8% currentColor mix, so it follows the tone automatically.

/* A re-skin changes the component tokens, never .alert itself */
.alert--notice { --alert-bg: var(--color-bg-subtle); --alert-fg: var(--color-fg-default);
                 --alert-border: var(--color-border-default); --alert-icon: var(--color-fg-muted); }

In dark the backgrounds become deep tinted surfaces (callaloo 950, mango 950, sorrel 950) and text and icons switch to the light end of each ramp, keeping text at 4.5:1 and icons at 3:1. The border stays decorative in both themes: the tint, the icon and the words carry the meaning.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

A <div> with a live-region role, following the APG Alert pattern:

  • role="alert" (assertive) only for danger that appears as the result of what the person just did or of an incident in progress: "We couldn’t sign you in just now", "Some things aren’t working right now". One per screen.
  • role="status" (polite) for everything else that appears or changes while the page is open: maintenance, unverified email, in review.
  • No role for an alert that is simply part of the page's content on first load and never changes (the expiring-certificate notice is fine either way). A role on static content is harmless; two role="alert"s on one screen are not.

The live region must exist before its text is inserted; render the empty container with the page and fill it, or screen readers will not announce it.

Keyboard

KeyAction
Tab / Shift+TabMoves through the alert's actions, then its dismiss button, in reading order. The container itself is not focusable.
Enter, SpaceActivates the focused action or dismisses.
EscNo effect. Alerts are not modal; Escape belongs to the dialog or sheet that has focus.

Focus

Alerts never take focus when they appear, with one exception: after a failed form submit, focus moves to the danger alert's title (given tabindex="-1") so the explanation is read first. After dismissing, focus moves to the start of #main, never to the top of the document. The dismiss button shows the shared ring on :focus-visible.

Labelling

The tone is never colour alone: the icon shape and the words carry it, so "Changes requested:" and "Your profile is suspended" say what kind of message it is. Icons are aria-hidden. The dismiss button needs an accessible name; prefer naming what is dismissed ("Dismiss maintenance notice") over a bare "Dismiss", which the mocks use and which is acceptable only when there is one dismissible alert on the screen.

Focus ring on canvas

The focus ring on a tinted alert background (for example pepper on sorrel 50) is not yet in the checked pairs; it is listed for addition. The 3px offset puts the ring partly over the tint, so the tone backgrounds must keep the ring at 3:1.

Motion

Alerts appear and disappear without animation. A banner that slid in would push the page down while someone reads; it is rendered with the page instead. The dismiss hover is a colour change only.

Touch

Banner dismiss buttons are 44px below 48rem. Inline alerts are not dismissible, so their 24px close never appears on phones. Link actions inside an alert keep the 24px minimum height of .btn--sm links with at least 8px between them.

Content

  • Title states the condition, not the category: "Your certificate expires on Monday 9 November 2026", not "Warning". No "Error:" or "Note:" prefixes; the icon does that job.
  • Text says what it means and what happens next, in one or two sentences: "That’s 30 days from today. Add your renewed certificate here to keep your profile published."
  • Reassure when nothing is lost: "New messages may take a few minutes to appear. Nothing you send is lost."; "Your email is still here; try again in a moment."
  • Say whose fault it is honestly: "Something went wrong on our side." Never blame the person for a server failure.
  • Quote the operator's reason verbatim in "Changes requested:" banners, in curly quotes, so the caterer reads exactly what was asked.
  • Actions are verb phrases that name the destination: "Upload a new certificate", "Go to photos", "Resend verification email", "View your page". Never "Click here" or "Learn more".
  • Dates in full on detail screens ("Monday 9 November 2026"), short in banners ("Sunday 18 October, 1:00–3:00 a.m."), always Toronto time; add "Toronto time" when the reader may be elsewhere.

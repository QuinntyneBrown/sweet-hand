Menu

A short list of actions or destinations that drops from one trigger. The account menu under the avatar is the one every signed-in person sees.

Overview

Use a menu when one trigger needs to offer two to seven actions or destinations that don't deserve permanent space. Opening it changes nothing; choosing an item does.

  • Account menu (.menu.account-menu) drops from the avatar in the site header. It shows who is signed in, then the person's routes and Sign out: Inbox, Account, Sign out for Marcia Thompson; Dashboard, Account, Sign out for Kendra Baptiste at Pelau House; Account, Sign out for operator Leah Mohammed.
  • Dropdown menu (.menu in a .menu-anchor) is the generic shape for row and toolbar actions on wide screens, such as an operator's "More actions for Pelau House". It supports section labels, dividers, checkable items (one sort order ticked), a danger item and keyboard-shortcut hints. Only the account menu ships in the mocks today; the rest is the approved shape for when a screen needs it.

When not to use a menu: for filters that update results (cuisine, area), use the filter trigger and its dropdown, which is a non-modal dialog with checkboxes, not a menu. To choose a form value, use a select. For site navigation on a phone, use the site menu sheet. For one or two actions, show buttons: the operator record shows Edit, Suspend and Remove as buttons because they fit.

Anatomy

  1. Panel--color-bg-surface-raised, 1px --color-border-default, --radius-md (--radius-lg for the account menu), --shadow-3, 4px padding, at least 12rem wide. A menu floats, so it is one of the few lifted surfaces.
  2. Section label (optional)Uppercase overline in --color-fg-subtle; role="presentation". Names a group or the object acted on.
  3. ItemIcon (optional, 20px) + label, 36px tall from 48rem and 44px below it (44px always in the account menu), --radius-sm, --text-body-sm.
  4. Shortcut (optional)<kbd> pushed to the end, 12px. Shown only for shortcuts that really exist.
  5. Divider1px --color-border-default with 4px above and below; role="separator".
  6. Danger item.menu__item--danger: sorrel text, sorrel tint on hover. Always last, after a divider; the ellipsis says a confirmation dialog follows.

Sizes

Two densities, chosen by where the menu lives, not by preference:

  • Compact (.menu): 36px items (--control-height-sm) from 48rem, --text-body-sm, 12rem minimum width. Below 48rem every .menu__item grows to 44px automatically (L2-065.1), so a dropdown opened on a phone is never compact.
  • Comfortable (.account-menu): 44px items (--target-comfortable), --text-body, --radius-md items, 18rem wide. For any menu a phone can open.

States

The open menu has one active item at a time: it gets the hover fill, and the keyboard-focused item adds an inset pepper ring. Hover and focus are forced with data-state; disabled and checked use aria-disabled and aria-checked.

Both themes

  • Sort enquiries
  • Sort enquiries
  • Open / closed: the trigger carries aria-expanded; the menu scales in from its anchor corner (scale-in, --duration-fast) and is removed from the DOM (or hidden) when closed.
  • Disabled items stay in place and focusable (aria-disabled="true"), so people learn the action exists. Explain why nearby if it isn't obvious.
  • Active (pressed) has no separate look: choosing an item closes the menu at once, then navigates, opens the confirmation dialog, or applies the choice.
  • Busy belongs to whatever the item starts (Sign out shows the browser's navigation; Suspend's dialog has its own busy state), never to the item.

Responsive behaviour

  • Account menu: position: fixed, its top tied to --layout-topbar-height (4rem, then 5rem from 48rem, 6rem from 62rem) so it always tucks under the header. It sits at --layout-margin from the right, min(18rem, 100vw − 2 × margin) wide, so at 320px it fills the width less 16px margins.
  • Below 48rem the customer header hides the avatar (.site-nav__extra); Inbox, Account and Sign out live in the site menu sheet instead. Caterer and operator headers keep the avatar on phones, so their account menu opens at phone width with 44px items.
  • Dropdown menus open below and aligned to the trigger's start edge; if they would overflow the viewport they flip up or to the end edge. They are never wider than the viewport minus both margins.
  • Below 48rem compact items grow to 44px (the phone-target rule in components.css); from 48rem they are 36px. A menu with more than six items becomes a sheet on phones instead.

Theming

The panel reads --color-bg-surface-raised, --color-border-default and --shadow-3; items read --color-fg-default and --color-bg-subtle on hover or focus; labels --color-fg-subtle; the tick --color-fg-accent; danger items --color-danger-fg and --color-danger-bg; the focus ring --color-focus-ring, drawn inset so the panel's edge never clips it.

In dark, the raised surface is stone-850, one step lighter than the stone-950 page, and the shadow deepens to read on black. The panel border still separates it if the shadow is lost (forced colours). A re-skin changes semantic tokens only; the menu has no component tokens.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

APG Menu Button. The trigger is a <button> with aria-haspopup="menu", aria-expanded and aria-controls. The list is role="menu", labelled by the trigger or by the account name; each <li> is role="none"; items are menuitem, menuitemcheckbox or menuitemradio. Items that navigate stay <a href>. Roving tabindex: only the active item has tabindex="0".

Keyboard

KeyAction
Enter, Space, ↓ on the triggerOpens the menu and focuses the first item. ↑ opens it on the last item.
↓ / ↑Next / previous item, wrapping at the ends. Disabled items are focusable; labels and dividers are skipped.
Home / EndFirst / last item.
A letterType-ahead: moves to the next item starting with that letter ("S" for Sign out).
Enter / Space on an itemActivates it and closes the menu. A checkable item toggles; a radio item checks itself and unchecks the others.
EscCloses the menu and returns focus to the trigger.
TabCloses the menu and moves focus on from the trigger. It never traps.

Focus

Opening moves focus into the menu. Closing by Esc, Tab, an outside click or choosing an item that does not navigate returns focus to the trigger. An item that opens a dialog hands focus to the dialog, and back to the trigger when the dialog closes. The focused item shows the fill plus a 2px inset ring (box-shadow, not outline), so it is visible even where the panel clips overflow.

Labelling

The trigger names the menu and its owner: "Account menu for Marcia Thompson", "More actions for Pelau House". The account menu's role="menu" is labelled by the name element. Icons are aria-hidden. A trailing ellipsis ("Suspend caterer…") means a dialog follows, and the item adds aria-haspopup="dialog". Shortcut hints are visible text; also expose them with aria-keyshortcuts on the item.

Disabled items (--color-fg-disabled) are exempt. The panel border is decorative; the shadow and surface step lift the menu, and forced-colours mode draws the border in CanvasText.

Motion

The panel scales from 96% and fades in over --duration-fast with --ease-enter, from the corner nearest its trigger (top left; top right for the account menu). Reduced motion zeroes the duration, so it simply appears. Closing is instant.

Touch

Below 48rem every menu item is 44px tall (L2-065.1). From 48rem compact items are 36px, stacked without gaps, which clears the 24px minimum (WCAG 2.5.8). Account-menu items are 44px at every width. A tap outside closes the menu without activating what was tapped.

Content

  • Items are short verb phrases or destination nouns in sentence case: "Inbox", "Account", "Sign out", "Edit details…", "View public page".
  • "Sign out" (two words), never "Log out" or "Logout".
  • An ellipsis marks an item that opens a dialog for more input or a confirmation: "Suspend caterer…", "Request changes…". Destinations and immediate actions have none.
  • Most common first, destructive last after a divider. Two to seven items; with more, split the menu or reconsider.
  • The account block's second line says what the person needs to know: the email (customer), business and email (caterer), role and sign-in method (operator).

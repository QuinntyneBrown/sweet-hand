Spinner

A spinner says "working on it" when the wait is short and has no shape to preview: the next photo in the viewer, a postal code lookup. It always comes with words for screen readers.

WCAG 2.2 AAStatus: stable.spinnerrole="status"

Overview

A spinner is a 2px ring with an ink arc that turns once every 800ms. It is the least informative loading indicator, so Sweet Hand uses it sparingly:

  • Inside the round photo placeholder when the photo viewer moves to a photo that has not loaded yet ("Loading photo 3 of 6").
  • Inline with a short status line while something small is checked: "Looking up M1B 2K3…" under the area search.
  • Inside a busy button, where it is drawn by the button itself (aria-busy="true"), not by a .spinner element.

Show it only after 300ms; most waits end sooner and a spinner that flashes is worse than none. Choose something else when:

  • Content is loading into a known layout (listing, inbox, queue, dashboard): a skeleton in the same shape, so nothing jumps.
  • The amount is measurable (an upload): a progress bar.
  • An action is in flight on a button: the button's own busy state.
  • Autosave is in flight: the save status line "Saving…" with its pulsing dot (see inline message).

Anatomy

  1. TrackFull circle, --border-width-thick (2px) in --color-border-default. Decorative.
  2. ArcThe top quarter in --color-fg-default (ink), turning linearly every --duration-loop-fast.
  3. Status textA role="status" wrapper with visible or visually hidden words ("Loading photo 3 of 6"). The ring itself is aria-hidden.

Variants

Standalone

Centred in the space the content will fill, with hidden text. In the viewer it sits inside a round placeholder exactly the size of the coming photo.

Inline with text

Small or medium spinner before a short status line. The words are visible, so nothing is hidden.

Looking up M1B 2K3…

Verifying your email…

.inline-status with .spinner--sm (16px) beside small text, default (20px) beside body text.
HTML

In a button (not a .spinner)

The button draws its own spinner in the label colour and keeps its label for screen readers. Never put a .spinner element inside a button.

Sizes

Match the spinner to the text or space beside it.

Small · 16px Medium · 20px Large · 40px
Small (--icon-size-sm) beside --text-body-sm; medium (--icon-size-md) beside body text; large (--space-10, 2px ring) inside photo placeholders and empty regions.

States

A spinner has no interactive states: it is either present (working) or removed (done). The matrix shows each size on each surface it appears on; with no real attribute to set, the "state" is the context.

Both themes

  • Delayed: render nothing for the first 300ms, then the spinner. If the wait ends between 300ms and 600ms, keep it for the remainder of 600ms so it never flickers.
  • Done: remove the spinner and its status text together; the arriving content announces itself (the photo's caption, the area name).
  • Taking long: after 10 seconds, replace the spinner with words and a way out ("Still loading. Try again"), using an empty state in error tone if it fails.

Responsive behaviour

  • Size never changes by breakpoint; a 20px ring reads the same on a phone and a 1440px screen.
  • Standalone spinners centre in the box the content will occupy (the round viewer photo is full width on xs, the lead photo size from 48rem), so the layout does not move when the content arrives.
  • Inline spinners stay on the first line of their text and do not wrap away from it (flex: none).

Theming

The ring reads --color-border-default; the arc reads --color-fg-default. In dark both flip with the theme: a pale arc on a dark-stone track. There are no component tokens. On the inverse surface (inside a toast, should one ever need it) give the parent color and override the arc with border-top-color: currentColor; the button already does this for its busy state.

The mock kit drew the arc in pepper red; it is ink now, because a red arc read as an error or a selected item on screens where pepper already marks the current filter.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

The ring is aria-hidden="true". The words live in a role="status" element (polite live region, WCAG 4.1.3) that exists before the text is inserted. The region being loaded gets aria-busy="true" until it is ready. There is no APG pattern for spinners; this follows the live region guidance of the APG and Understanding 4.1.3 Status Messages.

Keyboard

KeyAction
TabSkips the spinner; it is never focusable. Controls around it (Previous photo, Close photo viewer) stay operable while it turns.

Focus

A spinner never takes or moves focus. When the content arrives, focus stays where the person left it (on Next photo, for example).

Labelling

Say what is loading, not that something is: "Loading photo 3 of 6", "Looking up M1B 2K3…". The words are visually hidden for a standalone spinner and visible for an inline one; never both.

Contrast

Arc on surface
Arc on sunken well
Inline status text on page

The arc is a graphical object that conveys "working", so it needs 3:1 against the surface (WCAG 1.4.11); the track is decorative. The arc on the photo placeholder background (--color-bg-photo) is a checked pair too.

Motion

The spinner keeps turning under prefers-reduced-motion. It is not decoration: a still ring would read as frozen, and the turn is slow, linear, small and stops as soon as the content arrives (WCAG 2.3.3 covers motion triggered by interaction; this is a status indicator). Loop durations are deliberately not zeroed in tokens.css. Skeleton shimmer and the indeterminate progress sweep, which are decorative, do stop.

Touch

Nothing to touch, and nothing under the spinner is blocked: it never sits on top of a control.

Content

  • Name the thing: "Loading photo 3 of 6", "Looking up M1B 2K3…", "Verifying your email…". Not "Loading…" or "Please wait".
  • Present participle plus an ellipsis character (…) for visible text; no exclamation marks.
  • No time estimates unless they are real; if a wait passes 10 seconds, say so and offer a way out.
